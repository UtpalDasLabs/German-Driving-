## Cold open

> SCENE (sign): The deer-crossing warning sign, alone and large.
> 142

[curious] Picture a country road at dusk. Trees on both sides, headlights on. Something flashes past on the right: a red triangle, with a leaping deer on it.

[warm] You didn't really read it. You didn't have to. Before your brain had caught up, your foot had already come off the accelerator. That's not luck. That's design.

> SCENE (sign): Four shapes, four meanings.
> 101 | 250 | 209-30 | 357

[curious] German road signs speak a language of shapes and colours. A red triangle warns you. A red ring forbids something. A blue circle orders you to do something. And a rectangle gives you information. Learn the shapes, and you can read most signs before you can read what's on them.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Verkehrszeichen, Teil 1

[warm] This lesson is part one of two: the warning signs, the priority signs, and the signs that tell you what kind of place you've just driven into. Sixteen questions like the ones in the test.

## Triangles: danger ahead

> SCENE (term): The warning sign family.
> Gefahrzeichen | warning signs: a red triangle, point up | StVO § 40

[curious] The triangles are called [[Gefahrzeichen]], danger signs. Outside towns, they usually stand about 150 to 250 metres before the danger, so you have time to react. Inside towns, they're often much closer.

{Qsi15}

> SCENE (question): Question si15. The children warning sign.

[curious] Let's start with the one that matters most. What should you expect at this sign?

[countdown] Children suddenly running into the road. And children playing at the roadside. Both. This sign has no timetable. It doesn't switch off after school, at weekends, or in the holidays. So slow down and be ready to brake.

{Qsi16}

> SCENE (question): Question si16. The deer-crossing warning sign.

[curious] Back to our deer. What is true at this sign?

[countdown] Animals are especially likely at dusk and dawn. And where one animal crosses, others often follow. Deer tend to move in groups. If one has just jumped across in front of you, don't speed up in relief. Brake. The second one is probably on its way.

{Qsi14}

> SCENE (question): Question si14. The slippery-road sign, with a white plate underneath.

[curious] Now a triangle with a small white plate underneath. When does the warning given by these signs apply?

[countdown] Only when the road is wet. The triangle warns of skidding. The plate says [[bei Nässe]], when wet. The plate narrows the sign. We'll meet a lot more of these plates in part two.

{Qsi32}

> SCENE (question): Question si32. Red and white chevron boards at a bend.

[curious] Not every warning is a triangle. What do these red and white chevrons show?

[countdown] The direction of a bend. The arrows point the way the road goes. And where someone has gone to the trouble of putting them up, the bend is often tighter than it looks.

> SCENE (sign): The level-crossing warning and its three countdown markers.
> 151 | 153 | 156 | 159

[serious] One more warning, and it's an important one. Before a level crossing, you'll often see a triangle with a gate on it, then a row of posts with red stripes: three stripes, then two, then one. They count you down to the tracks.

{Qsi17}

> SCENE (question): Question si17. The three countdown markers.

[curious] Roughly how many metres before the level crossing is the three-stripe marker?

[countdown] 240 metres. Each stripe stands for about 80 metres. Three stripes, 240. Two, 160. One, 80. So when the last post goes by, the tracks are close, and a train always has right of way.

## Priority, one more time

> SCENE (sign): The two narrow-road signs, side by side.
> 208 | 308

[warm] You met the main priority signs in lesson one: the upside-down triangle, the stop sign and the yellow diamond. Here are two more, for a road that gets too narrow for two cars at once. One has a red arrow, the other a white one.

{Qsi30}

> SCENE (question): Question si30. A narrowing ahead, the sign at the blue car's side, an oncoming car.

[curious] You see the sign with the red arrow before a narrow section. What do you do?

[countdown] Give priority to oncoming traffic, waiting if necessary. The trick to remember it: the red arrow is you. Red means you wait. The drivers coming the other way see the blue sign with the white arrow, and they may go.

{Qsi13}

> SCENE (question): Question si13. The yellow diamond, crossed out.

[curious] And the yellow diamond with black lines through it. What does this sign show?

[countdown] End of the priority road. After it, the signs or right before left decide again. Often, a give-way triangle follows shortly after. It's a warning to switch from "I go" back to "I check".

## Places and zones

> SCENE (term): The information sign family.
> Richtzeichen | information signs: mostly rectangles | StVO § 42

[curious] The last family is the [[Richtzeichen]], the information signs. Most of them are rectangles. They tell you where you are. And often, where you are quietly changes the rules.

{Qsi11}

> SCENE (question): Question si11. The yellow town sign.

[curious] The big yellow sign with a town's name on it. What is the speed limit for a car from this sign on, unless signs say otherwise?

[countdown] 50 kilometres an hour. You don't need a separate speed sign. The yellow town sign, the [[Ortstafel]], is the speed sign. Inside a built-up area, 50 applies to every motor vehicle.

{Qsi10}

> SCENE (question): Question si10. The 30-zone sign.

[curious] Now a zone. What normally applies inside it?

[countdown] A maximum of 30 km/h. And at junctions, usually right before left. A 30 zone has no priority roads, so every junction becomes a little test of lesson one. The zone lasts until its end sign, however many junctions that takes.

{Qsi08}

> SCENE (question): Question si08. The traffic-calmed area sign.

[curious] This blue sign shows people, a child playing and a car, all on the same street. What applies inside this area?

[countdown] Drive at walking pace. Pedestrians may use the whole street and children may play anywhere. You park only in marked spaces. Germans call it a [[Spielstraße]], a play street. And the app's tip says it best: here, you're the guest.

{Qsi09}

> SCENE (question): Question si09. The same sign, crossed out: the end of the area.

[firm] And now, the way out. You're leaving the traffic-calmed area onto a normal road. Who has right of way?

[countdown] Everyone on the road I am joining. Coming out of a play street is like pulling out of a driveway. You let everyone go first and rule out any danger, even traffic from your left.

> SCENE (sign): The one-way street and no-entry signs.
> 220-20 | 267

[curious] Two signs that belong together. A blue arrow on a rectangle, and a red circle with a white bar. Most of the time, they're the same street, seen from opposite ends.

{Qsi06}

> SCENE (question): Question si06. The one-way street sign.

[curious] What applies in this street?

[countdown] I may only drive in the direction of the arrow. And I may also stop and park on the left. On a normal street, you park on the right. In a one-way street, both sides are allowed. Turning round, on the other hand, would mean driving against the flow.

{Qsi05}

> SCENE (question): Question si05. The red circle with the white bar.

[curious] And from the other end. What does this sign mean?

[countdown] No entry. It's the back door of that one-way street. You may drive along it, just not from here. And notice the shape: a red circle. Circles with red are bans, and part two is full of them.

{Qsi07}

> SCENE (question): Question si07. A blue sign with a white T, its top bar in red.

[curious] What does this sign tell you?

[countdown] A dead end. In German, a [[Sackgasse]]. The red bar is the end of the road. Expect to turn round, and don't follow the satnav blindly into it.

{Qsi23}

> SCENE (question): Question si23. The bus and tram stop sign.

[curious] The bus stop sign, with the green H on yellow. For how many metres before and after this sign may you not park?

[countdown] 15 metres. On both sides of the sign. A bus needs that room to pull in straight and pull out again, without its passengers stepping into the gutter.

{Qsi12}

> SCENE (question): Question si12. The motorway sign.

[curious] And the biggest place of all: the [[Autobahn]]. Who may use it?

[countdown] Motor vehicles built to go faster than 60 km/h. It's about what the vehicle was built for, not how fast you plan to drive. So a moped limited to 45 stays off, however brave its rider is. The same rule applies on expressways.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The shapes of this lesson.
> Dreieck | danger ahead @ triangle
> Rechteck | where you are @ rectangle
> Roter Pfeil | I wait @ red arrow
> Ortstafel | 50 from here @ town sign

[warm] So, that's part one. A triangle warns you, usually 150 to 250 metres before the danger outside towns. A rectangle tells you where you are, and that changes the rules. On a narrow road, the red arrow waits. And the yellow town sign means 50.
