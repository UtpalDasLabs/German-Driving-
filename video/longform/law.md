## Cold open

> SCENE (words): A night out, and the morning after.
> Party, Taxi, Sleep, Bakery

[curious] Picture this. Saturday night, a party. You do the sensible thing: you leave the car, and you take a taxi home at two. Then sleep.

[warm] Sunday, nine o'clock. You feel fine. You take the car to the bakery for [[Brötchen]].

[pause] Are you fit to drive? Hold that thought. We'll come back to that morning.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Promille & Punkte

[serious] This lesson is about what you bring into the car: alcohol, cannabis, medicine, tiredness, even your mood. And about the law around you: what you must do after a crash, what you must carry, and how points and probation work. Twenty questions, and the numbers matter.

## The numbers

{Qfi01}

> SCENE (question): Question fi01. Blood alcohol for new drivers.

[firm] Start with the easiest number to remember. How much alcohol may you have in your blood when you're on probation, or under twenty-one?

[countdown] 0.0 per mille. Not one small beer. During probation and before your twenty-first birthday, it's an absolute ban. Break it, and it's a serious probation offence: a course, and a longer probation period.

{Qfi02}

> SCENE (question): Question fi02. Blood alcohol for everyone else.

[curious] And for everyone else? From what blood alcohol level do you commit an offence, even with no signs of impairment?

[countdown] 0.5 per mille. From there, it's always an administrative offence: a fine, points and a driving ban. Even if you drive perfectly. And zero point eight is an old number: it hasn't been the limit since 2001.

> SCENE (stat): The three numbers.
> 0.3 | ‰ with driving errors: a crime @ point three
> 0.5 | ‰ always an offence @ point five
> 1.1 | ‰ always a crime @ one point one

[serious] Zero point five sits in the middle of three numbers. From zero point three, it's already a crime, if you make driving errors or show signs of impairment. And from one point one, you count as absolutely unfit to drive. That's always a crime, however steady you feel.

{Qfi03}

> SCENE (question): Question fi03. How fast alcohol leaves the body.

[curious] Now back to Sunday morning. Roughly how fast does the body break down alcohol?

[countdown] About 0.1 per mille per hour. And nothing speeds it up. Not strong coffee, not a cold shower, not fresh air. You just feel more awake while you're still drunk.

[warm] So say you went to bed at one point two. Seven hours later, your liver has cleared about zero point seven. Which leaves you at zero point five. On your way to the bakery.

{Qfi04}

> SCENE (question): Question fi04. Cannabis and driving.

[curious] Cannabis became legal for adults in Germany in 2024, and the driving rules changed with it. What has applied since then?

[countdown] From 3.5 ng/ml THC in blood serum it is an offence. That's three point five nanograms per millilitre. And during probation and under 21, any THC is forbidden. Same idea as alcohol: zero.

[firm] And the trap: mixing the two is never fine. Anyone combining cannabis and alcohol must be at zero point zero.

{Qfi05}

> SCENE (question): Question fi05. A packet of tablets.

[curious] You're taking medication. What do you do before driving?

[countdown] Read the leaflet and, when in doubt, ask a doctor or pharmacist. The [[Beipackzettel]] tells you if a medicine makes you drowsy. Plenty of over-the-counter cold and allergy remedies do. And alcohol on top usually makes it much stronger, not weaker.

## Body and mind

{Qfi06}

> SCENE (question): Question fi06. A licence with the code 01.

[curious] On some licences, there's a small code on the back, a [[Schlüsselzahl]]. Code 01 means vision correction. What does it mean for you?

[countdown] I must wear glasses or contact lenses when driving. On every journey, even the short ones. Without them, it's a fine, and trouble with your insurer after an accident. Spare glasses are a good idea, but they're not the rule.

{Qfi07}

> SCENE (question): Question fi07. A tired driver at night.

[curious] Last time, we met the microsleep. So how do you notice that you're too tired to drive?

[countdown] Frequent yawning and heavy eyelids. And I cannot remember the last few kilometres. Burning eyes, shivering, drifting in the lane. These are the warning signs. At the latest then, stop. Feeling especially alert isn't one of them.

{Qfi08}

> SCENE (question): Question fi08. A driver after an argument.

[curious] You're very angry after an argument. How does that affect your driving?

[countdown] I tend to drive more riskily and aggressively. And I notice hazards less well. Strong feelings distract you. Anger, grief, even joy. Calm down first. Then set off.

## The rule above all rules

> SCENE (term): The first paragraph of the StVO.
> Rücksicht | consideration | StVO § 1

[warm] Before any of the detailed rules, the traffic code starts with one idea: [[Rücksicht]], consideration. Paragraph one sits above everything else in it.

{Qla01}

> SCENE (question): Question la01. The basic rule of the StVO.

[curious] What does that basic rule in paragraph one demand?

[countdown] Constant care and mutual consideration. And nobody may be harmed, endangered, or obstructed or inconvenienced more than is unavoidable.

[firm] Which is why the third answer is wrong. You may have right of way, but if the other driver is plainly ignoring it, you don't force it. Being in the right doesn't help much in a hospital bed.

## After a crash

{Qla02}

> SCENE (question): Question la02. An accident with injured people.

[serious] You come across an accident with injured people. What do you do first?

[countdown] Secure the scene. Hazards, vest, triangle, so nobody else crashes into it. Then call 112 and give first aid. And driving on is not an option. Failing to help when you reasonably could is a crime: [[unterlassene Hilfeleistung]].

[warm] And you know more than you think. Everyone who gets a German licence does a first-aid course first. Secure, call, help. Doing something is always better than doing nothing.

{Qla03}

> SCENE (question): Question la03. A dent in a parked car; nobody around.

[firm] Now a quieter one, and it catches a lot of people. Pulling out of a space, you damage another car. The owner isn't there. What do you do?

[countdown] Wait a reasonable time and, if nobody comes, inform the police. The note under the wiper feels decent. But it isn't enough. Driving off without the facts being recorded is a crime, [[Unfallflucht]]. Even when the damage is small.

[warm] The rule to remember: a note is not a report.

## Papers, belts and insurance

> SCENE (term): A roadside check.
> Verkehrskontrolle | a police check | StVO § 36

[curious] Now the paperwork. It's less dramatic, but a [[Verkehrskontrolle]], a roadside police check, will test it. Here's what they'll ask for, and what's yours to answer for.

{Qla04}

> SCENE (question): Question la04. The papers in the glovebox.

[curious] Which documents must you carry when driving?

[countdown] My driving licence. And the registration certificate part I, the [[Fahrzeugschein]]. You hand both over when the police ask. Part II, the title document, stays at home. So does the purchase contract.

{Qla09}

> SCENE (question): Question la09. A car about to set off.

[curious] Who is responsible for the car being roadworthy when you set off?

[countdown] I am, as the driver. The car, its load and the people in it. The keeper is liable too, but that doesn't let you off. And the garage isn't in the car with you.

{Qla10}

> SCENE (question): Question la10. Seat belts, front and back.

[curious] Who has to wear a seat belt?

[countdown] Everyone in a seat that has a belt. Front and back, on every road. And as the driver, you're responsible for children being properly restrained. Not just on the motorway: a crash at fifty in town is plenty.

{Qla12}

> SCENE (question): Question la12. Car insurance.

[curious] Which insurance is compulsory for every registered car?

[countdown] Third-party liability insurance. The [[Haftpflicht]]. It pays for the damage you cause to others, so whoever you hit gets paid, even if you couldn't pay yourself. Cover for your own car, partial or comprehensive, is your choice.

## New drivers and points

{Qla05}

> SCENE (question): Question la05. The probation period.

[curious] Now, the rules for new drivers. The [[Probezeit]], probation. How many years does it normally last?

[countdown] 2 years from your first licence. But after one serious offence, category A, or two less serious ones, category B, you must attend a course, and probation is extended to four years.

{Qla06}

> SCENE (question): Question la06. A probation offence.

[firm] So which offence counts as serious, category A, during probation?

[countdown] More than 20 km/h over the limit. And running a red light. Others on the list: right-of-way violations, dangerous overtaking, alcohol or cannabis at the wheel. A single one means a course. Forgetting the hi-vis vest is a small fine. Not a probation offence.

{Qla11}

> SCENE (question): Question la11. A learner at the wheel, an adult beside them.

[warm] In Germany, you can start driving at seventeen, with a named adult beside you: [[Begleitetes Fahren ab 17]]. What must that companion have?

[countdown] Be at least 30 years old. Have held a class B licence for at least 5 years. And have no more than one point in the driving register. All three. And they must be named on the certificate. The 0.5 limit and the cannabis limit apply to them, too.

{Qla08}

> SCENE (question): Question la08. A traffic light that has been red for over a second.

[serious] You run a light that has been red for more than one second. What do you face?

[countdown] A one-month driving ban. And two points in the driving register. Plus two hundred euros. On probation, add a course and the extension. That's no caution.

{Qla07}

> SCENE (question): Question la07. The driving register.

[curious] Those points end up in Flensburg, in the [[Fahreignungsregister]]. At how many points is your licence withdrawn?

[countdown] 8 points. It works in steps, so you get a fair warning on the way.

> SCENE (list): The steps in Flensburg.
> 4–5 points | a reminder @ four
> 6–7 points | a warning @ six
> 8 points | the licence is gone @ eight

[warm] At four or five points, a reminder. At six or seven, a warning. And at eight, your licence is withdrawn.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The four rules of this lesson.
> 0.0 ‰ | probation and under 21 @ zero point zero
> 0.5 ‰ | the limit for everyone else @ point five
> Wait, then the police | a note is not a report @ you wait
> 8 points | the licence is gone @ eight

[warm] So, that's the law. Zero point zero while you're new or under twenty-one. Zero point five for everyone else. After a bump with nobody there, you wait, and then you tell the police. And eight points in Flensburg, and your licence is gone.
