## Cold open

> SCENE (term): A dark dashboard. One small red light.
> Rot | red means: stop now

[serious] Picture this. Late evening, the motorway, an hour from home. The radio's on, the road is empty. And then, on the dashboard, a small light comes on. It's [[rot]]. Red.

[curious] Most of us have the same first thought: it's probably nothing, I'll look at it tomorrow. Hold that thought. By the end of this lesson, you'll know why that's exactly the wrong one.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Fahrzeug & Umwelt

[warm] This lesson is about the car itself: tyres, warning lights, child seats, trailers. And about what your driving does to the air, the noise and your fuel bill. Twenty-five questions, and some of them carry four points.

## Before you drive

> SCENE (term): The tread depth.
> Profiltiefe | tread depth | § 36 StVZO

[curious] Start where the car touches the road. The grooves in your tyres, the [[Profiltiefe]], push water out of the way. Without them, a tyre on a wet road starts to float.

{Qve01}

> SCENE (question): Question ve01. The minimum tread depth.

[curious] So what's the legal minimum tread depth?

[countdown] 1.6 millimetres. That's the law. Experts recommend three millimetres for summer tyres and four for winter tyres, because below that, braking distances on wet roads and snow grow fast.

{Qve02}

> SCENE (question): Question ve02. Checking the tyre pressure.

[curious] And the air inside them. When do you check tyre pressure correctly?

[countdown] With the tyres cold. And to the maker's figures, raised for a full load. Both are right. Warm tyres read too high. You'll find the figures in the handbook, and often inside the fuel flap or on the driver's door.

{Qve15}

> SCENE (question): Question ve15. The head restraint.

[curious] Now, inside. How do you set the head restraint correctly?

[countdown] Top edge at about the top of your head, as close to it as possible. In a rear-end crash, it catches your head. Set too low, it works like a lever on your neck.

{Qve11}

> SCENE (question): Question ve11. What every car carries.

[curious] What must every car carry?

[countdown] A first-aid kit, a warning triangle, and at least one hi-vis vest. All three. One vest for everyone on board is better, kept inside the car, not in the boot.

{Qve14}

> SCENE (question): Question ve14. The main inspection.

[curious] And every car gets a regular check-up, the [[Hauptuntersuchung]], which most people just call "the TÜV". How often?

[countdown] Every two years; a new car first after three. The sticker on your rear number plate shows the month and year of the next one.

## When the dashboard talks

> SCENE (list): Two colours, two meanings.
> Rot | stop now @ red
> Gelb | garage soon @ amber

[firm] Back to that red light. Here's the rule for every warning light in every car: red means stop now. Amber means get to a garage soon.

{Qve03}

> SCENE (question): Question ve03. The red oil-pressure light.

[firm] So: you're driving, and the red oil-pressure warning light comes on. What do you do?

[countdown] Stop as soon as it is safe and switch off the engine. Without oil pressure, the engine isn't lubricated, and it can be wrecked within minutes. Then check the oil level.

[serious] Carrying on home is exactly the thought from the beginning. And revving higher doesn't pump more oil. It just wrecks the engine faster.

{Qve04}

> SCENE (question): Question ve04. Emergency braking with ABS.

[curious] Your car has ABS. Something jumps out in front of you. How do you brake?

[countdown] Stamp on the brake and keep it pressed. And yes, you can steer during the full stop. That's what ABS is for: the wheels don't lock. The pedal pulses and rattles under your foot. That's normal. Stay on it. Pumping the brake only made sense in cars without ABS.

{Qve05}

> SCENE (question): Question ve05. Electronic stability control.

[curious] And its cousin, ESP. What does it do?

[countdown] It helps prevent skids by braking individual wheels, and by cutting power. What it can't do is change physics. Too fast into a bend is still too fast.

{Qve06}

> SCENE (question): Question ve06. Daytime running lights.

[firm] Lots of cars have daytime running lights now. When must you switch on dipped headlights anyway?

[countdown] At dusk and in the dark. And in tunnels and in poor visibility. Daytime lights shine only at the front, barely light the road, and the rear lights often stay off. So from behind, you can be very hard to see.

## Children on board

> SCENE (term): The airbag.
> Airbag aus | airbag off

[serious] Now, the most important minute in this lesson. An airbag opens in a fraction of a second, with enormous force. For an adult facing forward, that's protection. For a baby facing backwards, it's something very different.

{Qve12}

> SCENE (question): Question ve12. A rear-facing child seat in the front.

[serious] You want to fit a rear-facing child seat in the front passenger seat. What must you do?

[countdown] Switch off the passenger airbag. A deploying airbag hits the back of that seat with enormous force. It can kill the child.

[firm] Not "probably", not "on a long trip". Every time. Rear-facing seat in front means [[Airbag aus]]. If your car has a light that confirms it, check it before you drive off.

{Qve13}

> SCENE (question): Question ve13. Child seats and height.

[curious] And when can a child stop using a child seat? Children under twelve must use one until they reach what height?

[countdown] 150 centimetres. Twelve years or 150 centimetres, whichever comes first. Until then, only a suitable, approved child seat.

## Loads, trailers and towing

{Qve08}

> SCENE (question): Question ve08. Luggage on the roof.

[curious] Holiday time. You want to carry luggage on the roof. What do you bear in mind?

[countdown] Not to exceed the permitted roof load in the vehicle papers. And the car handles differently: higher centre of gravity, more sensitive to crosswind. Loose items are never fine, however slowly you drive. A load must stay put, even in an emergency stop.

{Qve09}

> SCENE (question): Question ve09. Trailers on a class B licence.

[firm] And if the roof isn't enough, a trailer. Careful, this one catches people. Which trailers may you tow on a class B licence?

[countdown] Trailers up to 750 kg gross weight. And heavier trailers, if the whole combination is at most 3,500 kg gross. What your car is rated to tow doesn't decide it. Your licence does.

> SCENE (stat): The trailer limits.
> 750 | kg trailer @ seven hundred and fifty
> 3500 | kg the whole combination @ three thousand five hundred

[warm] So: seven hundred and fifty kilos for the trailer, or three thousand five hundred for car and trailer together. With a short training course, code ninety-six, you can go up to four thousand two hundred and fifty. Beyond that, you need class [[BE]].

{Qve07}

> SCENE (question): Question ve07. Towing with a rope.

[curious] Breakdown. A friend tows your car with a rope. What is correct?

[countdown] Both vehicles switch on their hazard lights. And the gap between the vehicles is no more than 5 m, with the rope flagged in the middle. The trap: the ignition in the towed car stays on. Off, the steering lock can engage, and steering and brakes become very heavy.

{Qve10}

> SCENE (question): Question ve10. A steep descent.

[curious] And in the mountains: a long, steep hill down. How do you spare the brakes?

[countdown] Use a low gear and engine braking. Brake all the way down, and the brakes overheat until they barely work. Going down, use the gear you'd need going up.

[serious] And never switch off the ignition to save fuel on a hill. You lose power steering and brake assistance, exactly when you need them.

## Quiet cars, thirsty cars

{Qve16}

> SCENE (question): Question ve16. An electric car at low speed.

[curious] Electric cars. What do you need to bear in mind when driving one slowly?

[countdown] Pedestrians can barely hear it and may not expect me. And it accelerates very hard from standstill. Children and blind people especially notice a quiet car late. So in car parks and side streets, you do the noticing for them.

> SCENE (term): Anticipating.
> Vorausschauend fahren | look ahead, save fuel

[warm] Now the environment, and your wallet. Almost everything here comes down to two words: [[vorausschauend fahren]]. Driving with foresight.

{Qec01}

> SCENE (question): Question ec01. Driving economically.

[curious] How do you drive most economically?

[countdown] Change up early and keep revs low. And anticipate and avoid needless braking. Every stop you didn't need throws away energy you paid for at the pump.

{Qec02}

> SCENE (question): Question ec02. Coasting to a red light in gear.

[curious] You see a red light ahead and take your foot off, but leave the car in gear. What happens?

[countdown] The fuel supply is cut off: overrun fuel cut-off, in German [[Schubabschaltung]]. Modern engines use no fuel at all then. Shift into neutral, and the engine burns fuel just to idle.

{Qec06}

> SCENE (question): Question ec06. Fuel use at high speed.

[curious] On the motorway. Why does fuel consumption climb so steeply at high speed?

[countdown] Air resistance grows with the square of speed. Twice the speed, four times the air resistance. Doing 120 instead of 160 often saves around a third of the fuel, and costs you about twelve minutes per hundred kilometres.

{Qec05}

> SCENE (question): Question ec05. What raises fuel use.

[curious] What increases fuel consumption?

[countdown] An empty roof rack. Tyres under-inflated. And the air conditioning. All three. The rack adds drag, soft tyres add rolling resistance, and the air conditioning takes engine power.

{Qec09}

> SCENE (question): Question ec09. Hard journeys.

[curious] Which journeys are hardest on the engine and the environment?

[countdown] Short trips with a cold engine. A cold engine uses much more fuel, wears faster, and the catalytic converter hasn't warmed up yet.

[dry amusement] Two kilometres to the bakery? The bakery has a bicycle rack.

## Noise, fumes and the city

> SCENE (fact): The law on noise and fumes.
> § 30 StVO | Unnötiger Lärm ist verboten. | Unnecessary noise and avoidable exhaust are forbidden.

[serious] This one isn't just good advice. It's the law: [[unnötiger Lärm ist verboten]]. Unnecessary noise and avoidable exhaust.

{Qec03}

> SCENE (question): Question ec03. Forbidden noise and exhaust.

[curious] So what exactly is forbidden?

[countdown] Warming the engine up while stationary. And pointlessly driving up and down in town. Both. The engine may keep running at a red light, though for a long wait it pays to switch off.

{Qec04}

> SCENE (question): Question ec04. A closed level-crossing barrier.

[curious] Which brings us here. You face a long wait at a closed level-crossing barrier. What do you do?

[countdown] Switch off the engine. The same in a long queue or at roadworks lights. And revving it to charge the battery only adds noise and fumes.

> SCENE (sign): The low-emission zone sign.
> 270.1

[curious] In many German cities, you'll meet this sign: [[Umweltzone]], the low-emission zone.

{Qec08}

> SCENE (question): Question ec08. The low-emission zone sign.

[curious] Who may drive into a low-emission zone?

[countdown] Vehicles with the required emissions sticker. Nearly everywhere today, that's the green one, the [[grüne Plakette]], on the windscreen. Without it, you can be fined, however slowly you drive.

{Qec07}

> SCENE (question): Question ec07. Used engine oil.

[curious] Last one. You've changed your oil yourself. Where does the old oil go?

[countdown] At a collection point, such as where I bought the oil. One litre of used oil can contaminate a million litres of drinking water. And shops that sell engine oil must take the same amount back, free of charge.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The four things from this lesson.
> Rot heißt halt | red light: stop, engine off @ red light
> Airbag aus | rear-facing seat in front @ airbag
> 750 / 3,500 kg | trailers on class B @ trailers
> Vorausschauend | early gears, few stops @ foresight

[warm] So, that's your car. Four things to keep. A red light means stop now, engine off. A rear-facing child seat in front means airbag off. Trailers on class B: seven fifty, or three and a half tonnes together. And foresight saves fuel, brakes and nerves.
