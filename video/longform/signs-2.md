## Stopping and parking

{Qsi02}

> SCENE (question): Question si02. The no-stopping sign with the red cross.

[curious] So, back to the bakery. What is allowed on the carriageway at this sign?

[countdown] Nothing, stopping is completely forbidden. Not for a minute, not with the engine running, not even to let someone out. This is the [[absolutes Haltverbot]], the absolute no-stopping sign.

{Qsi03}

> SCENE (question): Question si03. The same sign, but with a single red stripe instead of a cross.

[curious] Now one stripe instead of a cross. What does this sign allow?

[countdown] Stopping for up to three minutes. And stopping longer to load or to let passengers in and out. What it doesn't allow is parking. The app's tip: a cross means never, a single stripe means briefly. So for the bakery, you'd want the stripe.

## Red rings: you must not

> SCENE (term): The regulatory sign family.
> Vorschriftzeichen | regulatory signs: red ring = must not, blue = must | StVO § 41

[curious] Both of those belong to the [[Vorschriftzeichen]], the regulatory signs. Most of them are round. If the ring is red, it forbids something. What's inside the ring tells you what.

{Qsi04}

> SCENE (question): Question si04. A plain white circle with a red ring.

[curious] Let's start with the emptiest sign of all. Just a white circle with a red ring. What does this sign mean?

[countdown] No vehicles of any kind, a bicycle may be pushed. Nothing's drawn inside, because no vehicle at all may drive in. But push your bike, and you count as a pedestrian again.

{Qsi24}

> SCENE (question): Question si24. A ban on motor vehicles, with a white plate underneath.

[curious] Now a ban on motor vehicles, with a plate underneath: [[Anlieger frei]]. Who may still drive in, despite the ban?

[countdown] Residents and those with business there, such as visitors and deliveries. An [[Anlieger]] isn't only someone who lives there. It's anyone who has a reason to be on that street. What the sign keeps out is the shortcut.

> SCENE (sign): The two overtaking bans.
> 276 | 277

[curious] Now two bans that look almost the same. One shows a red car beside a black car. The other, a red lorry beside a black car. The red vehicle is the one that may not overtake.

{Qsi18}

> SCENE (question): Question si18. The overtaking ban with two cars.

[curious] Whom may you still overtake at this sign?

[countdown] A cyclist. And a motorbike without a sidecar. The ban covers vehicles on more than one track. Anything on two wheels in a single line, like bicycles, mopeds and motorbikes, you may still pass, with enough room.

{Qsi19}

> SCENE (question): Question si19. The overtaking ban for lorries.

[firm] And the lorry version, with a trap. You're driving a car with a trailer, over three and a half tonnes together. May you overtake at this sign?

[countdown] Yes, cars are exempt from this ban. It's for heavy motor vehicles, and tractors. Cars and buses are expressly left out, with a trailer or without one. So the weight of the whole combination doesn't make your car a lorry.

> SCENE (sign): The signs that end restrictions.
> 278-60 | 280 | 282

[curious] And how do bans end? With these: white circles with black diagonal stripes, often with the old restriction greyed out underneath. They're the "you're free now" signs.

{Qsi01}

> SCENE (question): Question si01. A 50 speed limit.

[firm] A favourite trap. Where does this speed limit end?

[countdown] Where a sign shows its end, such as sign 278 or 282. A junction doesn't end it, and neither does a fixed distance. There are only two exceptions. A plate under the sign says how long it lasts. Or the limit belongs to a warning sign, and it obviously ends where the danger does.

{Qsi20}

> SCENE (question): Question si20. The end-of-all-restrictions sign.

[curious] And the sign with stripes only, nothing else on it. What does this sign cancel?

[countdown] Every restriction along the stretch before it, such as speed limits and overtaking bans. After it, the general rules apply again. Outside towns, for a car, that's usually 100.

## Blue circles: you must

> SCENE (term): Orders, in blue.
> Blau heißt: muss | blue means must | StVO § 41

[curious] Now flip the colour. [[Blau heißt: muss]]. Blue means must. A blue circle doesn't forbid anything. It tells you what to do, or where you have to go.

{Qsi21}

> SCENE (question): Question si21. A blue circle with a white 30.

[firm] And that makes this one sneaky. A number in a blue circle. What does this sign require?

[countdown] Do not drive slower than 30 km/h unless traffic, visibility or weather require it. It's a minimum speed, not a maximum. A red ring around the 30 would mean "no faster". Blue means "at least". And vehicles that can't manage it may not use the road.

{Qsi22}

> SCENE (question): Question si22. Three blue signs: a bicycle; a pedestrian and bicycle shared; and the two split.

[curious] Three blue circles with bicycles on them. Who may use these paths?

[countdown] Cyclists, and, on the two right-hand ones, pedestrians. The first is a cycle path. The second, a shared path for walking and cycling. The third, a split path, each side for one group. Never your car. And e-scooters belong on the cycle path too.

## The small print

> SCENE (term): The extra plates.
> Zusatzzeichen | extra plates: they narrow or extend the sign above | StVO § 39

[curious] The white rectangles under a sign are called [[Zusatzzeichen]], extra plates. You've seen two already: when wet, and access only. They're the small print, and the test loves small print.

{Qsi25}

> SCENE (question): Question si25. The no-stopping sign, with a plate showing a time.

[curious] Our bakery sign again, with a plate underneath that says 16 to 18. When does this no-stopping rule apply?

[countdown] Only between 4 pm and 6 pm. The plate narrows the main sign. Outside those two hours, this sign doesn't stop you. Read the plate before you decide.

> SCENE (sign): The two distance plates.
> 1001-30 | 1004-30

[curious] Now two plates that look alike, and mean something quite different. One says [[auf 800 m]]. The other says [[nach 200 m]].

{Qsi31}

> SCENE (question): Question si31. A 70 speed limit with the plate "auf 800 m".

[curious] This one stands under a speed limit. What does this plate mean?

[countdown] The limit applies for the next 800 metres. [[Auf]] tells you how long. [[Nach]] tells you how far until something starts. And because the plate already gives the length, no end sign follows.

## Lines on the road

> SCENE (sign): A solid line and a broken line.
> 295 | 340

[warm] Last chapter. The lines painted on the road are signs as well. And the most important ones come down to a simple idea: a solid line is a wall, a broken line is a door.

{Qsi26}

> SCENE (question): Question si26. A solid white line between the lanes.

[curious] What does this solid line mean?

[countdown] It must not be crossed. And you must not drive on it either. Not to overtake, not to turn, not even with two wheels over it, however clear the road ahead looks.

{Qsi27}

> SCENE (question): Question si27. A broken white line between the lanes.

[curious] And the broken line. What applies to it?

[countdown] I may cross it if nobody is put in danger. It's a guide line, there to divide the lanes. For overtaking, for example, you may cross it, as long as the traffic allows it.

{Qsi28}

> SCENE (question): Question si28. A hatched area with diagonal white stripes.

[curious] What does this marking mean?

[countdown] Hatched area, it must not be driven on. And not to stop or park on, either. It's a solid line that has been coloured in. Treat it as part of the wall.

{Qsi29}

> SCENE (question): Question si29. A white zigzag line at the kerb.

[curious] And the very last sign question. What does this zigzag marking show?

[countdown] An area where stopping or parking is forbidden. You'll see it in front of exits and at bus stops. It marks a no-stopping or no-parking area, or makes an existing one longer.

## What to take with you

> Roter Ring | must not @ red ring
> Blauer Kreis | must @ blue circle
> Zusatzzeichen | the small print @ small print

[warm] So, that's the signs, all of them. A red ring means must not. A blue circle means must. The plate underneath is the small print, so always read it. And on the road, a solid line is a wall, a broken line is a door.
