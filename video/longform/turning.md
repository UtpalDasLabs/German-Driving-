## Cold open

> SCENE (traffic): The junction from the cyclist question: the blue car waits to turn right, indicator on; a cyclist rides up the cycle lane beside it.
> tu01

[curious] Picture a green light in a German city. You're turning right. Indicator on, mirror checked, and the road you're turning into is empty. Everything looks fine.

[serious] Except for the one thing you can't see. A cyclist on the cycle lane beside you, going straight on, riding quietly into the space just behind your door pillar. Your mirrors don't cover it. Only your eyes do, if you turn your head.

[warm] That turn of the head has a name in German, and by the end of this lesson it'll be a reflex. Today: turning, reversing and parking. Twenty-two questions, and a lot of everyday driving.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Abbiegen & Parken

[curious] Some of these rules save lives at junctions. Some just save you a ticket outside the bakery. Both kinds come up in the test, so let's take them in order.

## Turning right

> SCENE (term): The turning rule.
> Abbiegen | turning | StVO § 9

[curious] [[Abbiegen]], turning. Most of it lives in paragraph nine of the road traffic regulations, and it starts with where you put the car.

{Qtu08}

> SCENE (question): Question tu08. Turning right.

[curious] First, the simple one. How do you turn right?

[countdown] Keep as far right as possible. Stay close to the kerb and take the corner in a tight arc, so you never swing across the street you're turning into.

[dry amusement] And not signalling because the road looks clear? It only looks clear. That's rather the point of this lesson.

{Qtu10}

> SCENE (question): Question tu10. When to signal.

[firm] Which brings us to the indicator. When do you use it?

[countdown] In good time before turning. Also before every lane change, and when you pull away from the kerb. An indicator announces what you're about to do. Switching it on halfway round the corner announces nothing. By then, everyone's found out.

{Qtu01}

> SCENE (question): Question tu01. The blue car turns right; a cyclist rides straight on along the cycle lane.

[curious] Now, the scene from the beginning. You want to turn right, and a cyclist is riding straight on along the cycle path to your right. What do you do?

[countdown] Let the cyclist go first. And look over my right shoulder before turning. Both are right. A cyclist going straight on in the same direction goes before you, on the road or on a cycle path beside it.

[serious] And you'll only see them with that look over your shoulder: the [[Schulterblick]]. Turning right means a right shoulder check. Every single time. Forget it in the practical test, and that alone can fail you.

{Qtu02}

> SCENE (question): Question tu02. The blue car turns right; a pedestrian and a child are crossing the road it turns into.

[curious] You're turning, and people are walking across the road you're turning into. What now?

[countdown] Wait until they have crossed. When you turn, pedestrians get your special care. Edging through the gaps between them isn't patience, it's pressure. And the horn doesn't make anyone walk faster. Least of all a child.

{Qtu09}

> SCENE (question): Question tu09. A tram beside you, going straight on.

[firm] One more, and it surprises anyone who's never driven in a city with trams. You want to turn. A tram beside you is going straight on, in the same direction. Who goes first?

[countdown] The tram. Turning vehicles let rail vehicles go first, even when they're running alongside you the same way. A tram can't swerve, and it can't stop quickly.

[dry amusement] And signalling first doesn't win you anything. You can't out-indicate a tram.

## Turning left

> SCENE (traffic): The blue car waits to turn left, an oncoming car going straight on.
> pr04

[curious] Turning left is more work, because you cross other people's paths. First you move over. Then you look. Twice.

{Qtu03}

> SCENE (question): Question tu03. Positioning to turn left.

[curious] So, how do you position yourself to turn left?

[countdown] On a two-way road, up to the centre line. In a one-way street, as far left as possible. Both are right, and they're the same idea: move as far left as the road allows, in good time. That leaves the space on your right for everyone going straight on.

> SCENE (term): The double look behind.
> doppelte Rückschau | the double look behind

[warm] Now the [[doppelte Rückschau]], the double look behind. Once before you move over, and once more right before you turn. Why twice? Because in those few seconds, someone behind you may have decided to overtake.

{Qtu04}

> SCENE (question): Question tu04. Turning left, step by step.

[curious] So what is part of turning left?

[countdown] Signalling and positioning in good time. Checking the mirror before moving over. And one more look behind just before turning. All three. Mirror, signal, position, shoulder check, turn. Say it a few times, and it becomes one movement.

## Driveways, turning round, reversing

> SCENE (manoeuvre): Turning the car round in the road, forwards and back.
> turnround

[curious] Now the slow manoeuvres: turning into a driveway, turning the car round, reversing. They happen at walking pace, so they feel harmless. The law sees it the other way round. It holds them to its strictest standard.

{Qtu05}

> SCENE (question): Question tu05. Turning off the road into a driveway.

[curious] You want to turn off the road into a driveway. What applies?

[countdown] Any danger to others must be ruled out. And if necessary, I get someone to guide me. In German, [[Gefährdung ausgeschlossen]]. Not "be careful": nobody at all may be put at risk. The same goes for turning round and reversing. The junction rules still apply, but they're not the whole story.

{Qtu06}

> SCENE (question): Question tu06. The no-U-turn sign.

[firm] Where is turning round forbidden?

[countdown] On motorways and expressways. And wherever sign 272 stands, the crossed-out U: [[Verbot des Wendens]]. But not in every street in town. Elsewhere, you may turn round, as long as nobody is endangered.

[dry amusement] Missed your exit on the motorway? Take the next one. Reversing back to it is forbidden too, and a spectacularly bad idea.

{Qtu07}

> SCENE (question): Question tu07. Reversing out of a parking space with little view behind.

[curious] You're reversing out of a parking space, and you can see very little behind you. What do you do?

[countdown] Go slowly, keep looking and get someone to guide me if needed. The reversing camera helps, but it doesn't replace your eyes. It can miss a low post, or a small child right behind the bumper. And hurrying so you hold nobody up just leaves you less time to react.

## Stopping or parking?

> SCENE (sign): The two no-stopping signs.
> 283 | 286

[curious] Now, parking. German has two words for leaving your car at the roadside, and the signs care a lot about the difference. [[Halten]] is stopping. [[Parken]] is parking.

[warm] The red cross means no stopping at all: [[absolutes Haltverbot]]. The single red bar is an [[eingeschränktes Haltverbot]]: you may stop briefly, but not park. So where exactly is the line?

{Qpa01}

> SCENE (question): Question pa01. Stopping or parking.

[curious] When are you parking, rather than just stopping?

[countdown] When I leave the vehicle. Or when I stop for more than three minutes. Either one makes it parking. So hopping out to post a letter is parking, even if it takes thirty seconds. And waiting in a queue, or at a red light? That's neither.

{Qpa02}

> SCENE (question): Question pa02. Where stopping is forbidden.

[curious] Then where is even stopping forbidden?

[countdown] At narrow places and where visibility is poor. And on level crossings. Add sharp bends, slip roads and fire-brigade access points. The pattern: anywhere a stopped car would block the road, or hide a danger.

[dry amusement] Outside a bakery, though, you're fine. The law has nothing against bread.

{Qpa03}

> SCENE (question): Question pa03. The zebra crossing sign.

[curious] How many metres before a zebra crossing may you not stop?

[countdown] 5 metres. Not on the crossing, and not in the five metres before it. A car stopped right up to the stripes is a wall, and a child stepping out from behind it can't be seen until it's too late.

{Qpa04}

> SCENE (question): Question pa04. Parking near a junction.

[curious] And junctions. How many metres before and after a junction may you not park, if there's no separate cycle track?

[countdown] 5 metres, before and after, measured from where the kerb lines meet. Where there is a separate cycle track on the right, it's eight metres before the junction.

{Qpa05}

> SCENE (question): Question pa05. Where parking is forbidden.

[curious] Where is parking forbidden?

[countdown] In front of driveway entrances and exits. And in front of dropped kerbs. On a narrow road, even opposite a driveway, so the car inside can still get out. And the dropped kerb, the [[abgesenkter Bordstein]], is there for wheelchairs and prams. Park in front of it, and you've closed someone's door.

{Qpa12}

> SCENE (question): Question pa12. The pavement and a fire-brigade access.

[curious] And where may you not park?

[countdown] On the pavement, unless a sign or marking allows it. And in front of a fire-brigade access point. The pavement belongs to people on foot. And at a fire-brigade access, you may not even stop. If there's a fire, those seconds belong to someone else.

{Qpa11}

> SCENE (question): Question pa11. A car parked in the second row.

[firm] Now, a classic. Just for a moment, hazard lights on. May you park in the second row?

[countdown] No. Double-parking is forbidden, and the hazard lights don't change that. They're a warning, not a permit. Meanwhile everyone behind you has to swing out into oncoming traffic to get past.

{Qpa07}

> SCENE (question): Question pa07. Which side of the road.

[curious] On which side of the road may you normally park?

[countdown] On the right. And on the left in one-way streets, or where there are tracks on the right. But never on the left just because there's room. You'd cross the oncoming lane to get in, and again to get out.

## The parking disc

> SCENE (term): The parking disc.
> Parkscheibe | parking disc

[curious] [[Parkscheibe]], the parking disc. The little blue dial behind your windscreen. Where a sign allows parking with a disc, you set it to the time you arrived. Well, almost.

{Qpa06}

> SCENE (question): Question pa06. Setting the parking disc.

[curious] You arrive at ten past nine and set your parking disc. What time do you set?

[countdown] 9:30. You always round up to the next half hour. Arrive at ten past nine, and the disc says half past.

[dry amusement] One of the rare German rules that rounds in your favour. Enjoy it.

## Getting out

> SCENE (traffic): A row of parked cars; a cyclist rides up alongside the blue car.
> pa08

[serious] And now the most dangerous moment of parking: getting out. A cyclist riding past the parked cars, and a door swinging open right in front of them. In German, that crash is simply called [[Dooring]].

{Qpa08}

> SCENE (question): Question pa08. Opening the driver's door with a cyclist behind.

[curious] You want to open the driver's door. How do you best protect cyclists?

[countdown] Open it with my right hand, so I turn and see behind. Reaching across with your far hand turns your whole body, and your eyes land right on the blind spot. The Dutch reach, or in German, [[holländischer Griff]]. The mirror alone can miss a cyclist.

{Qpa09}

> SCENE (question): Question pa09. Leaving the parked car.

[curious] What do you do before leaving your parked car?

[countdown] Switch off the engine and apply the parking brake. And secure it against rolling away and against unauthorised use: windows up, doors locked, key with you. Leaving the engine running while you nip into a shop is forbidden.

{Qpa10}

> SCENE (question): Question pa10. Parked at night, outside town.

[curious] Last one. You park at the edge of the road at night, outside town. What must you do?

[countdown] Light the vehicle, for example with sidelights. Outside town, a parked car in the dark must be lit. Reflectors only work once someone's headlights are already on you, and the rear fog light would dazzle. In town, the parking light on the traffic side is enough, and where streetlights stay on all night, you usually need none.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The four rules of this lesson.
> Schulterblick | before every turn @ shoulder
> Doppelte Rückschau | turning left: look twice @ twice
> Gefährdung ausgeschlossen | reversing and turning round @ nobody
> Drei Minuten | then stopping is parking @ three minutes

[warm] So, that's turning, reversing and parking. A shoulder check before every turn. A double look behind when you turn left: you look twice. When you reverse or turn round, nobody may be put at risk. And after three minutes, or the moment you step out, stopping becomes parking.

[curious] Twenty-two questions done. And next time someone opens a car door in front of you, you'll know exactly which hand they should have used.
