## Cold open

> SCENE (traffic): The four-way junction from the order question: a blue car, a white car opposite, a cyclist from the right. The arrows show where each wants to go. Then they drive.
> pr03 | move @ one after the other

[curious] Three vehicles reach the same junction at the same moment. No traffic lights. No signs. No police officer. Nobody waves anybody through.

[warm] In a lot of countries, this is where people start making eye contact, flashing their lights, inching forward to see who blinks first.

[firm] In Germany, nobody needs to blink. There is exactly one right order, and the law already knows it. Watch: one after the other.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Vorfahrt

[serious] This is [[Vorfahrt]], right of way. Mistakes here are among the most common causes of crashes on German roads. In the theory test, these are the five-point questions. Get two of them wrong, and you've failed, however well you did on everything else.

[warm] So let's make sure that doesn't happen. By the end of this video, you'll know every rule that decides who goes first. There are fewer than you think.

## Right before left

> SCENE (term): One rule, large.
> rechts vor links | right before left | StVO § 8

[curious] It all starts with three words: [[rechts vor links]]. Right before left. Wherever nothing else decides it, with no sign, no light and no officer, the vehicle coming from your right goes first.

{Qpr01}

> SCENE (question): Question pr01. The blue car going straight, a white car from the right.

[curious] First question. You want to go straight on. There are no signs at this junction. Who has right of way?

[countdown] The car from the right. Going straight on doesn't help you, and being there first doesn't either.

[dry amusement] And neither does having the bigger car. Your SUV is not a legal argument.

{Qpr02}

> SCENE (question): Question pr02. Now the white car comes from the left.

[curious] Now flip it. Same junction, but the other car comes from your left. Do you have to let it go first?

[countdown] No, it comes from the left and has to let me go. This time it's yours.

[warm] But here's the habit good drivers have: they take their right of way gently. You can't see into the other driver's head. Rolling in carefully costs you one second. A crash costs a lot more.

{Qpr16}

> SCENE (question): Question pr16. A T-junction. The blue car is on the road that carries straight on; a car waits in the side road on the right.

[firm] Now, careful. This next one catches almost everybody. You're on the road that carries straight on. A car is waiting in a side road on your right. There are no signs. Who has right of way?

[countdown] The car coming out of the side road on the right. I know. It feels wrong. Your road is wider, it's straighter, it's obviously the main road.

[serious] But "obviously" isn't in the law. A road only becomes a priority road when a sign says so. Without a sign, a T-junction is right before left, just like any crossroads. Plenty of real crashes in quiet residential streets happen exactly like this.

> SCENE (list): Three things that do not give you priority.
> Wider road | it is not a sign @ wider
> Going straight on | turning changes nothing @ straight
> Getting there first | there is no queue @ first

[warm] So, three things that feel like they should give you priority, and don't. Being on the wider road. Going straight on. And getting there first. Junctions in Germany don't work like a queue at the bakery.

{Qpr03}

> SCENE (question): Question pr03. The three vehicles from the opening, numbered 1 to 3.

[curious] Remember the junction from the beginning? No signs, everyone going straight on. In what order do they go?

[countdown] 2, 3, 1. Here's the trick. Look for the vehicle with nobody on its right. That's the white car, number 2, so it goes first. The cyclist, number 3, only had to wait for car 2. And you, number 1, had the cyclist on your right, so you go last.

> SCENE (term): The method, as one line.
> Who has nobody on the right? | that one goes first

[warm] That's the whole method for every "in which order" question. Find the vehicle whose right-hand side is clear. It goes. Then ask the same question again with whoever's left.

{Qpr20}

> SCENE (question): Question pr20. The blue car wants to turn left; car 2 comes from the right, going straight.

[curious] Let's try it. No signs. You want to turn left, and car 2 is coming from your right. In what order?

[countdown] 2, 1. Turning doesn't switch the rule off. Car 2 is on your right, its path crosses yours, so it goes first.

{Qpr13}

> SCENE (question): Question pr13. A side road — a farm track — meets a road. A car comes from the left.

[curious] Now, there is one exception, and it's a fun one. You come out of a field track, a [[Feldweg]], onto a road with no signs. A car is coming from your left. Who has right of way?

[countdown] The car. Coming out of a field or forest track, you never have right of way. Not even over traffic from the left.

[dry amusement] The tractor coming out of the cornfield waits for everyone. That's the law, and honestly, it's fair.

## Signs that settle it

> SCENE (sign): The three priority signs, side by side.
> 205 | 206 | 306

[curious] Most busy junctions in Germany don't leave it to right before left. A sign decides. And these three have unique shapes, so you can recognise them from behind, or covered in snow.

[warm] The triangle standing on its point: [[Vorfahrt gewähren]], give way. The red octagon: [[Halt. Vorfahrt gewähren.]] Stop, then give way. And the yellow diamond: [[Vorfahrtstraße]], the priority road.

{Qpr19}

> SCENE (question): Question pr19. A T-junction with the give-way sign. A motorbike comes from the left.

[curious] You want to turn right, and the triangle stands at your road. What does it mean for you?

[countdown] I must give way to traffic from the left. Turning right, you slide into the very lane that traffic from your left is already using. The motorbike goes first.

{Qpr06}

> SCENE (question): Question pr06. The same give-way sign. The blue car wants to turn left; cars come from both sides.

[curious] Same sign, but now you want to turn left. Who do you let go first?

[countdown] The car from the left, and the car from the right. Both of them. Turning left, you cross one lane and join the other. Everyone on the main road goes before you.

{Qpr07}

> SCENE (question): Question pr07. The stop sign and a white stop line.

[firm] And this one. What must you do at this sign?

[countdown] Stop at the stop line, even if the road is clear. And give way. That's two right answers, and in the exam you have to tick both.

[serious] Stop means the wheels stand completely still. Rolling over the line is a classic way to fail the practical test. Your examiner will be watching your wheels.

{Qpr08}

> SCENE (question): Question pr08. The blue car on a road with the yellow diamond; a car waits in the side road on the right, behind a give-way sign.

[warm] Now you're the lucky one, on the priority road with the yellow diamond. A car waits on your right. You're going straight on. Who has right of way?

[countdown] I do. The sign beats right before left. The car on your right has the triangle, so it waits.

{Qpr17}

> SCENE (question): Question pr17. A lorry on the priority road; the blue car and a white car face each other on the side roads, both behind give-way signs. The white car wants to turn left.

[curious] Let's put it together. A lorry on the priority road. You and another car facing each other on the side roads, both with the triangle. The other car wants to turn left. In what order do they go?

[countdown] 3, 1, 2. The lorry is on the priority road, so it goes first. Then it's just you and the other car, facing each other. And between the two of you, a different rule decides: the one turning left waits. We'll get to that rule in a minute.

{Qpr09}

> SCENE (question): Question pr09. A white triangle with a black vertical bar crossed by a thin horizontal one.

[firm] One more sign, and people mix it up with the diamond all the time. How far does the priority from this sign reach?

[countdown] Only to the next junction. This sign gives you priority once, at the next junction, and that's it. For priority along a whole road, you need the yellow diamond.

> SCENE (term): The difference, side by side.
> 301 einmal · 306 dauerhaft | 301 is once, 306 is all the way

[warm] An easy way to remember it: the triangle is a single favour. The diamond is a long-term relationship.

## Turning

> SCENE (term): The turning rule.
> Linksabbieger warten | left-turners wait | StVO § 9

[curious] The second big rule isn't about right before left at all. It's about turning: [[Linksabbieger warten]]. Whoever turns left lets oncoming traffic go first. Always.

{Qpr04}

> SCENE (question): Question pr04. The blue car signals left; the oncoming car goes straight.

[curious] You want to turn left. The car coming towards you wants to go straight on. Who goes first?

[countdown] The oncoming car. And it doesn't matter who put their indicator on first. An indicator tells people what you're planning. It doesn't give you any rights.

{Qpr18}

> SCENE (question): Question pr18. Both cars are on the priority road.

[firm] And now, the trap. You're on the priority road, the yellow diamond, and you want to turn left. The car coming the other way is on the priority road too. Who has to wait?

[countdown] I do. The priority road only settles things with the side roads. Between two cars on the same priority road, the turning rule decides, and the left-turner waits.

{Qpr05}

> SCENE (question): Question pr05. Both cars signal left, facing each other.

[curious] What if you both want to turn left? How do you turn?

[countdown] In front of each other, each turns ahead of the other. You pass on the inside, like two people stepping aside in a corridor. Only when the layout or the traffic makes that impossible do you go around each other.

## When the priority road bends

> SCENE (traffic): A junction where the yellow priority road bends from the south to the west. The blue car follows the bend; a pedestrian waits at the corner.
> pr10

[curious] Sometimes the priority road doesn't go straight through a junction. It bends. A small extra sign under the diamond shows the shape: the thick line is the priority road, the thin lines are the side roads.

{Qpr10}

> SCENE (question): Question pr10. The blue car follows the bend to the left; a pedestrian at the corner.

[curious] You're following the priority road as it bends to the left. What must you do?

[countdown] Signal left, and watch out for pedestrians crossing the road. You never actually leave the priority road, but the law treats following the bend like turning. So you signal, and if someone's crossing, you wait.

{Qpr11}

> SCENE (question): Question pr11. The priority road bends from west to north. The blue car is on the side road from the south, with a give-way sign. Car 1 follows the bend.

[curious] Now you're on the side road, and the priority road bends right in front of you. You want to go straight on. Who do you let go first?

[countdown] Car 1. It's on the priority road, following the bend, and its path crosses yours. Going straight on gives you no priority at all.

## Roundabouts and driveways

> SCENE (sign): The roundabout sign with the give-way sign above it.
> 215 | 205

[curious] Roundabouts. Here's the surprise: in Germany, a circle is only legally a roundabout if it has this blue sign, with three arrows going anticlockwise. Usually there's a give-way triangle above it.

{Qpr14}

> SCENE (question): Question pr14. A roundabout; the blue car waits at the entry, a car already on the ring.

[curious] So what applies at this roundabout?

[countdown] Vehicles already on the roundabout have right of way. No indicator when you go in. And signal right when you leave. All three are right. In without indicating, out with it.

{Qpr15}

> SCENE (question): Question pr15. The same circle — but no signs at all.

[firm] Now the same circle, but no sign anywhere. Who has right of way?

[countdown] I do, the car on the circle is coming from my left. Without the blue sign, the law doesn't see a roundabout. It sees an ordinary junction with an island in the middle. And an ordinary junction means right before left.

[dry amusement] Yes, that means the cars already going round have to wait for you. It feels strange. It's the law.

{Qpr12}

> SCENE (question): Question pr12. A car about to pull out of a driveway.

[warm] Last one. You're pulling out of a driveway onto the road. What applies?

[countdown] I must let traffic on the road go first, and any danger to others must be ruled out. Out of a driveway, a car park or a parking spot at the kerb, you have priority over nobody. And if you can't see, ask someone to guide you out. Nobody will laugh.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The four rules of this lesson.
> Rechts vor links | where nothing else decides @ right before left
> Schilder gehen vor | signs beat the rule @ signs
> Linksabbieger warten | left-turners wait @ left
> Kein Schild, kein Kreisverkehr | no sign, no roundabout @ no blue sign

[warm] So, that's right of way. Four rules. Right before left, wherever nothing else decides. Signs beat that rule. Left-turners wait for oncoming traffic. And without the blue sign, a circle is just a junction.

[curious] Twenty questions done. That's a whole block of five-pointers you don't have to fear anymore.
