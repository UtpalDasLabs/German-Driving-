## Cold open

> SCENE (traffic): The four-way junction from the order question: a blue car, a white car opposite, a cyclist from the right. The arrows show where each wants to go. Then they drive.
> pr03 | move @ one after the other

[dramatic] Three vehicles arrive at the same junction at the same moment. No traffic lights. No signs. No police officer. Nobody waves anybody through.

[dramatic] And yet, in Germany, there is exactly one right answer to who goes first, who goes second and who waits until last. Watch them go, one after the other.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Vorfahrt

[dramatic] This lesson is about [[Vorfahrt]] — right of way. It is the topic that fails more people in the theory test than any other, because these are the five-point questions. Get two of them wrong, and the test is over, however well you did on the rest.

## Right before left

> SCENE (term): One rule, large.
> rechts vor links | right before left | StVO § 8

[dramatic] Everything starts with three words: [[rechts vor links]]. Right before left. Where nothing else decides it — no sign, no light, no officer — the vehicle coming from your right goes first.

{Qpr01}

> SCENE (question): Question pr01. The blue car going straight, a white car from the right.

[dramatic] You want to go straight on. There are no signs at this junction. Who has right of way?

[countdown] The car from the right. It does not matter that you are going straight, or that you are the bigger car, or that you got there first. Right before left.

{Qpr02}

> SCENE (question): Question pr02. Now the white car comes from the left.

[dramatic] Now turn it around. Same junction, but the other car comes from your left. Do you have to let it go first?

[countdown] No — it comes from the left and has to let me go. You have the right of way. But drive as if the other driver might not know that. You cannot see into their head.

{Qpr16}

> SCENE (question): Question pr16. A T-junction. The blue car is on the road that carries straight on; a car waits in the side road on the right.

[dramatic] Here is the one that catches almost everybody. You are driving along the road that carries straight on. A car is waiting in a side road on your right. There are no signs. Who has right of way?

[countdown] The car coming out of the side road on the right. A road that is wider, or straighter, or busier is not a priority road. Only a sign makes it one. Without a sign, a T-junction is right before left, like any other.

> SCENE (list): Three things that do not give you priority.
> Wider road | it is not a sign @ wider
> Going straight on | turning changes nothing @ straight
> Getting there first | there is no queue @ first

[dramatic] So, three things that feel like they should give you priority, and do not: being on the wider road, going straight on, and getting there first.

{Qpr03}

> SCENE (question): Question pr03. The three vehicles from the opening, numbered 1 to 3.

[dramatic] Back to the opening. No signs, and everyone is going straight on. In what order do the vehicles go?

[countdown] 2, 3, 1. Look for the vehicle with nobody on its right: that is the white car, number 2, so it goes first. The cyclist, number 3, only has to wait for car 2. And you, number 1, have the cyclist on your right, so you go last.

> SCENE (term): The method, as one line.
> Who has nobody on the right? | that one goes first

[dramatic] That is the whole method for every "in which order" question. Find the vehicle whose right-hand side is clear. It goes. Then ask the question again.

{Qpr20}

> SCENE (question): Question pr20. The blue car wants to turn left; car 2 comes from the right, going straight.

[dramatic] No signs, and this time you want to turn left, while car 2 comes from your right. In what order do the vehicles go?

[countdown] 2, 1. Turning does not change right before left. Car 2 is on your right and crosses your path, so it goes first.

{Qpr13}

> SCENE (question): Question pr13. A side road — a farm track — meets a road. A car comes from the left.

[dramatic] One exception to right before left. You come out of a farm track — a [[Feldweg]] — onto a road with no signs. A car is coming from the left. Who has right of way?

[countdown] The car. Whoever comes out of a field or forest track never has right of way — not even over traffic from the left.

## Signs that settle it

> SCENE (sign): The three priority signs, side by side.
> 205 | 206 | 306

[dramatic] Most junctions in Germany do not leave it to right before left. A sign decides. And the shapes are chosen so that you can read them even from behind, or covered in snow.

[dramatic] The triangle standing on its point: [[Vorfahrt gewähren]], give way. The red octagon: [[Halt. Vorfahrt gewähren.]] Stop, then give way. And the yellow diamond: [[Vorfahrtstraße]], the priority road.

{Qpr19}

> SCENE (question): Question pr19. A T-junction with the give-way sign. A motorbike comes from the left.

[dramatic] You want to turn right, and this sign stands at your road. What does it mean for you?

[countdown] I must give way to traffic from the left. Turning right, you join the very lane that traffic from your left is driving in. The motorbike goes first.

{Qpr06}

> SCENE (question): Question pr06. The same give-way sign. The blue car wants to turn left; cars come from both sides.

[dramatic] Same sign, but now you want to turn left. Whom must you let go first?

[countdown] The car from the left, and the car from the right. Both. Turning left, you cross one lane and join the other. Everyone on the main road goes before you.

{Qpr07}

> SCENE (question): Question pr07. The stop sign and a white stop line.

[dramatic] And at this sign: what must you do?

[countdown] Stop at the stop line, even if the road is clear, and give way. Two right answers, and in the exam you have to tick both. Stop means the wheels stand still. Rolling slowly over the line is one of the most common reasons people fail the practical test, too.

{Qpr08}

> SCENE (question): Question pr08. The blue car on a road with the yellow diamond; a car waits in the side road on the right, behind a give-way sign.

[dramatic] Now you are the one on the priority road, with the yellow diamond. A car is waiting on your right. You are going straight on. Who has right of way?

[countdown] I do. Here, the sign beats right before left. The car on your right has the triangle. It waits.

{Qpr17}

> SCENE (question): Question pr17. A lorry on the priority road; the blue car and a white car face each other on the side roads, both behind give-way signs. The white car wants to turn left.

[dramatic] Put it all together. A lorry on the priority road. You and another car facing each other on the side roads, both with the triangle. The other car wants to turn left. In what order do the vehicles go?

[countdown] 3, 1, 2. The lorry is on the priority road, so it goes first. Then you and the other car are left facing each other — and between the two of you, a different rule decides. The one turning left waits. We will come to that rule in a moment.

{Qpr09}

> SCENE (question): Question pr09. A white triangle with a black vertical bar crossed by a thin horizontal one.

[dramatic] One more sign, and it is easy to confuse with the diamond. How far does the priority given by this sign reach?

[countdown] Only to the next junction. This is [[Vorfahrt]] — priority, at the next junction, and that is all. For priority along a whole road, you need the yellow diamond.

> SCENE (term): The difference, side by side.
> 301 einmal · 306 dauerhaft | 301 is once, 306 is all the way

## Turning

> SCENE (term): The turning rule.
> Linksabbieger warten | left-turners wait | StVO § 9

[dramatic] The second big rule is not about right before left at all. It is about turning. Whoever turns left lets oncoming traffic go first. Always.

{Qpr04}

> SCENE (question): Question pr04. The blue car signals left; the oncoming car goes straight.

[dramatic] You want to turn left. The car coming towards you wants to go straight on. Who goes first?

[countdown] The oncoming car. It does not matter who signalled first. Left-turners wait for oncoming traffic.

{Qpr18}

> SCENE (question): Question pr18. Both cars are on the priority road.

[dramatic] And now the trap. You are on the priority road, the yellow diamond, and you want to turn left. The car coming the other way is on the priority road too. Who has to wait?

[countdown] I do. The priority road only settles things with the side roads. Between two cars on the priority road, the turning rule decides — and the left-turner waits.

{Qpr05}

> SCENE (question): Question pr05. Both cars signal left, facing each other.

[dramatic] What if you both want to turn left? How do you turn?

[countdown] In front of each other - each turns ahead of the other. You pass each other on the inside, like two people stepping aside in a corridor. Only if the junction's layout or the traffic makes that impossible do you go around each other.

## When the priority road bends

> SCENE (traffic): A junction where the yellow priority road bends from the south to the west. The blue car follows the bend; a pedestrian waits at the corner.
> pr10

[dramatic] Sometimes the priority road does not go straight through a junction. It bends. A small extra sign under the diamond shows the shape: the thick line is the priority road, the thin lines are the side roads.

{Qpr10}

> SCENE (question): Question pr10. The blue car follows the bend to the left; a pedestrian at the corner.

[dramatic] You are following the priority road as it bends to the left. What must you do?

[countdown] Signal left, and watch out for pedestrians crossing the road. Even though you never leave the priority road, the law treats following the bend as turning. You signal, and if someone is crossing, you wait.

{Qpr11}

> SCENE (question): Question pr11. The priority road bends from west to north. The blue car is on the side road from the south, with a give-way sign. Car 1 follows the bend.

[dramatic] Now you are on a side road, and the priority road bends right in front of you. You want to go straight on. Whom do you let go first?

[countdown] Car 1. It is on the priority road, following the bend, and its path crosses yours. Going straight on gives you no priority at all.

## Roundabouts and driveways

> SCENE (sign): The roundabout sign with the give-way sign above it.
> 215 | 205

[dramatic] A roundabout in Germany is a roundabout only if it has this blue sign, with three arrows going anticlockwise. Usually it stands under a give-way sign.

{Qpr14}

> SCENE (question): Question pr14. A roundabout; the blue car waits at the entry, a car already on the ring.

[dramatic] What applies at this roundabout?

[countdown] Vehicles already on the roundabout have right of way. Do not signal when entering. And signal right when leaving. All three are right. In without indicating, out with it.

{Qpr15}

> SCENE (question): Question pr15. The same circle — but no signs at all.

[dramatic] Same circle, but there is no sign anywhere. Who has right of way?

[countdown] I do - the car on the circle is coming from my left. Without the blue sign, this is not a roundabout in the eyes of the law. It is an ordinary junction with an island in the middle — and an ordinary junction is right before left.

{Qpr12}

> SCENE (question): Question pr12. A car about to pull out of a driveway.

[dramatic] Last one. You are pulling out of a driveway onto the road. What applies?

[countdown] I must let traffic on the road go first, and any danger to others must be ruled out. Leaving a driveway, a car park or the kerb, you have no priority over anyone. If you cannot see, get someone to guide you out.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The four rules of this lesson.
> Rechts vor links | where nothing else decides @ right before left
> Schilder gehen vor | signs beat the rule @ signs
> Linksabbieger warten | left-turners wait @ left
> Kein Schild, kein Kreisverkehr | no sign, no roundabout @ no blue sign

[dramatic] So: right before left, wherever nothing else decides. Signs beat the rule. Left-turners wait for oncoming traffic. And without the blue sign, a circle is just a junction.
