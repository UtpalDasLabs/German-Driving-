## Cold open

> SCENE (traffic): A motorway: a lorry with its hazard lights on blocks the blue car's lane ahead; two cars in the lane to the left.
> ba02

[curious] Picture a motorway, a kilometre before some roadworks. A sign says your lane is ending. Some drivers move over straight away and join the long queue on the left. Others carry on in the emptying lane, right up to where it ends.

[dry amusement] The first group is quite sure the second group is jumping the queue. You can see it on their faces.

[firm] In Germany, the law is on the side of the second group. By the end of this video, you'll know why.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Grundregeln & Überholen

[warm] This lesson is about the ground rules: where on the road you drive, how you change lanes, when you may use the horn. And then the manoeuvre where a mistake usually means a head-on collision: overtaking. Twenty questions, and two of them are worth five points.

## Keep right

> SCENE (term): The first rule of the road.
> Rechtsfahrgebot | keep right | StVO § 2

[curious] The first rule is one word in German: [[Rechtsfahrgebot]]. The duty to keep right. Germany drives on the right, of course. But the rule asks for more than that.

{Qba01}

> SCENE (question): Question ba01. Where on the road you drive.

[curious] Which side of the road do you drive on?

[countdown] As far right as reasonably possible. Not only when someone's coming towards you, or wants to pass. On an empty road too, and over the top of a hill, where you can't see what's coming.

[dry amusement] The middle of the road isn't a safe distance from the edge. It's where the oncoming car will be.

{Qba06}

> SCENE (question): Question ba06. A town road with two marked lanes in the same direction.

[firm] Now an exception, and it surprises people. In town, on a road with two marked lanes in your direction: may you pick either lane?

[countdown] Yes, in a car. In town, with marked lanes, vehicles up to three and a half tonnes may choose their lane freely. It's called [[freie Fahrstreifenwahl]]. What you may not do is weave from lane to lane to get ahead.

## Changing lanes

{Qba03}

> SCENE (question): Question ba03. A car about to move into the next lane.

[curious] Before you move over into another lane, what must you do?

[countdown] Check the mirror and over your shoulder. And signal in good time. Two right answers. Your mirrors leave a blind spot beside and just behind you, big enough to hide a motorbike. Only a turn of the head covers it: the [[Schulterblick]].

[dry amusement] A short toot isn't on the list. We'll come to the horn in a minute.

> SCENE (term): The zip.
> Reißverschlussverfahren | the zip merge | StVO § 7

[curious] Back to those roadworks. The German word for what should happen there is a long one: [[Reißverschlussverfahren]]. The zip. Two lanes close into one, tooth by tooth.

{Qba02}

> SCENE (question): Question ba02. The blue car's lane is blocked ahead; cars in the lane to the left.

[curious] Your lane is ending. How do you change lanes?

[countdown] Zip-merge, just before the narrowing. You drive on to where your lane really ends, and then it's one car from the left, one from the right. The drivers in the continuing lane must let that happen. Moving over a kilometre early isn't polite. It just turns two short queues into one long one.

## The horn and the hazard lights

{Qba04}

> SCENE (question): Question ba04. When the horn is allowed.

[curious] When may you use the horn?

[countdown] To warn others of danger. And outside town, to announce that you're overtaking. Those two, and that's it. The same goes for flashing your headlights.

[dry amusement] Greeting a friend, waking up the car in front at a green light, letting off steam: none of those count. In Germany, a toot is a warning, and people hear it as one.

{Qba05}

> SCENE (question): Question ba05. When the hazard lights are allowed.

[curious] And the hazard lights, the [[Warnblinklicht]]. When may you switch them on?

[countdown] When I have broken down or am being towed. And to warn others of danger, for example at the tail of a queue. When traffic suddenly stops on the motorway, they tell the people behind you: brake, now.

[firm] But stopping in the second row "just for a minute" with the hazards on is still illegal parking. Blinking lights don't change that.

## Eyes far ahead

> SCENE (term): The habit good drivers share.
> vorausschauend fahren | drive with anticipation

[warm] Good drivers share one habit: [[vorausschauend fahren]]. Driving with anticipation. Seeing trouble before it reaches you.

{Qba08}

> SCENE (question): Question ba08. A road stretching far ahead.

[curious] What's part of driving with anticipation?

[countdown] Looking far ahead, not just at the car in front. And checking the mirrors regularly. Watch only the bumper ahead, and you see the brake lights three cars further on too late. Driving close to react faster gets it backwards. Being close is exactly what takes your reaction time away.

{Qba07}

> SCENE (question): Question ba07. The car in front signals left but stays where it is.

[firm] And now a phrase you'll meet again and again: an unclear traffic situation, [[unklare Verkehrslage]]. What does it mean?

[countdown] I cannot reliably tell what others will do next. Say the car in front is signalling left, but it isn't moving over. Is it turning? Did it forget the indicator? You don't know. And when you don't know, you don't overtake, and you get ready to brake. Night or a wide road doesn't make it unclear. People do.

## When not to overtake

> SCENE (sign): The no-overtaking sign.
> 276

[serious] Which brings us to overtaking. Sometimes a sign forbids it: this one, the [[Überholverbot]]. But mostly there's no sign, and you decide. That's where it goes wrong.

{Qov01}

> SCENE (question): Question ov01. When overtaking is forbidden.

[firm] Five points on this one. When is overtaking forbidden?

[countdown] When the traffic situation is unclear. And when I am not much faster than the vehicle I want to pass. If you're only a little faster, you spend ages side by side, on the wrong side of the road. Oncoming traffic far away, on the other hand, is exactly what you want to see before you pull out.

## Overtaking, step by step

{Qov06}

> SCENE (question): Question ov06. The steps of an overtake.

[curious] So you've decided it's safe. What must you do when overtaking?

[countdown] Signal left and look over your shoulder before pulling out. And the other two as well: signal right when you go back in, and don't cut in on the car you passed. Overtaking is two lane changes, and each gets the full routine. A good habit: pull back in once you can see the whole car you passed in your rear-view mirror.

{Qov10}

> SCENE (question): Question ov10. A lorry ahead on a country road.

[curious] The hardest thing to overtake on a country road is a lorry. What must you be ready for?

[countdown] The overtaking distance is very long. And yes, the other two as well. A lorry is long and often only a little slower than you, so passing it can take several hundred metres. Behind it, you see almost nothing of the oncoming traffic. And as you come out of its shelter, a crosswind can push you sideways.

{Qov03}

> SCENE (question): Question ov03. The blue car is being overtaken.

[firm] Now flip it around. You're the one being overtaken. What must you not do?

[countdown] Speed up. The other driver has planned their overtake around your speed. Accelerate, and you make it longer, with them still on the wrong side of the road. If anything, ease off and keep right.

[dry amusement] Even if it's a very small car, and you take it personally.

## Cyclists: one and a half, and two

> SCENE (traffic): The blue car behind a cyclist on a straight road.
> ov02

[curious] Overtaking someone on a bike has its own rule, and it comes in metres.

{Qov02}

> SCENE (question): Question ov02. The blue car behind a cyclist, outside town.

[curious] Outside town, what's the minimum side clearance when you overtake a cyclist?

[countdown] 2 metres. In town, it's one and a half. The same goes for people on foot and on e-scooters. A pothole or a gust of wind, and a cyclist can swerve a long way.

> SCENE (term): The two numbers.
> innerorts 1,5 m · außerorts 2 m | in town 1.5 m, outside 2 m | StVO § 5

[warm] So: [[innerorts]], in town, one and a half metres. [[außerorts]], outside town, two. If it doesn't fit, you don't overtake.

{Qov12}

> SCENE (question): Question ov12. A cyclist ahead, an oncoming car, no room.

[firm] Five points again. You're behind a cyclist. There's oncoming traffic, and no room for the one and a half metres. What do you do?

[countdown] Stay behind the cyclist until I can pass with enough room. The clearance is the law, not a suggestion. And the horn is no way out. The cyclist has nowhere to go, and a toot is for warning of danger, not for creating it.

[warm] Usually it's a few seconds of waiting. That's the whole price.

## Passing on the right

> SCENE (term): The rule.
> links überholen | overtake on the left | StVO § 5

[curious] In Germany, you overtake on the left: [[links überholen]]. But there are a few places where passing on the right isn't just allowed. It's the right thing to do.

{Qov04}

> SCENE (question): Question ov04. The car in front signals left and has moved to the middle; the blue car behind it.

[curious] The car in front of you is signalling left and has moved towards the middle of the road. How do you get past?

[countdown] On the right, if there's enough room. It has clearly shown that it's turning left. Passing on the left would put you exactly where it's about to go.

{Qov09}

> SCENE (question): Question ov09. A tram on a two-way street.

[curious] A tram, on an ordinary two-way street. How do you overtake it?

[countdown] On the right. The tracks usually run down the middle, and on the left is the oncoming traffic. Only in a one-way street, or where the tracks lie too far to the right, do you pass on the left.

{Qov11}

> SCENE (question): Question ov11. A town road with several marked lanes.

[curious] Remember the free choice of lanes in town? Here's what follows from it. When may you pass other vehicles on the right in town?

[countdown] On roads with several marked lanes in one direction. If you may choose your lane, the right one can simply be the faster one. But a gap on the right is not a lane.

{Qov07}

> SCENE (question): Question ov07. The motorway; the left lane crawls in a queue.

[firm] And the motorway. The left lane is crawling along in a queue, and yours on the right is moving. May you go faster on the right?

[countdown] Yes, at most 20 km/h faster and with great care, if the left lane is doing 60 km/h or less. That's the line the courts have drawn. On a free-flowing motorway, overtaking on the right is still forbidden. But in slow traffic, you don't have to brake just to stay behind the left lane.

## Where nobody overtakes

{Qov05}

> SCENE (question): Question ov05. Places where overtaking is forbidden.

[curious] Where is overtaking forbidden, even with no ban sign in sight?

[countdown] Approaching and on zebra crossings. And from the warning signs before a level crossing until you're over the tracks. There, motor vehicles don't overtake motor vehicles, from the first warning sign until the rails are behind you. Bridges, though, have no general rule. Only a sign would forbid it there.

> SCENE (sign): The two signs to remember.
> 350 | 151

[warm] So, two signs. The [[Fußgängerüberweg]], and the warning for a [[Bahnübergang]]. Where you see them, you stay in line.

{Qov08}

> SCENE (question): Question ov08. A lorry stands on the blue car's side, hazard lights on; a car comes the other way.

[curious] Last one. There's an obstruction on your side of the road, and traffic is coming the other way. Who has to wait?

[countdown] I do. Whoever has the obstruction on their side lets the oncoming traffic through. It doesn't matter who got there first. And when you pull out around it, you signal.

[dry amusement] It's the zip all over again: there's an order, and nobody needs to fight about it.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The four rules of this lesson.
> Rechts fahren | even on an empty road @ keep right
> Reißverschluss | at the end, one by one @ zip
> Links überholen | and only when it's clear @ overtake
> 1,5 m · 2 m | for cyclists @ cyclists

[warm] So, the ground rules. Keep right, even when the road is empty. Merge like a zip, at the end, one by one. Overtake on the left, and only when it's clear. And give cyclists one and a half metres in town, two outside.

[curious] Twenty questions done, both five-pointers included.
