## Cold open

> SCENE (term): A licence card, and a new address.
> Umschreibung | converting your licence

[warm] Picture this. You've been driving for fifteen years. School runs, night drives, motorways, a city where the traffic never stops. Then you move to Germany, and someone asks you: have you done your [[Umschreibung]] yet? Converted your licence?

[curious] It sounds like starting from zero. It isn't. For many people, it's mostly paperwork. For some, it's one or two tests. And your years behind the wheel count for a lot in both.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Führerschein umschreiben

[warm] This lesson is for everyone who already drives and now lives here. Where your licence comes from decides almost everything, so let's start there.

## From the EU or the EEA

> SCENE (list): The countries whose licences simply stay valid.
> EU | all member states @ EU
> EWR | plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway @ Iceland

[warm] If your licence comes from a country in the EU or the EEA, that's the EU plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, the good news is short. A valid licence stays valid in Germany. You don't need to convert it.

[curious] One small thing. If your licence has an expiry date, you renew it at the German licensing office once you live here. Everything else stays as it is.

## Everyone else: six months

> SCENE (stat): The deadline.
> 6 | months from the day you take up residence

[curious] With a licence from any other country, you have [[sechs Monate]], six months. They start on the day you take up residence in Germany: the day you start living here, not the day you first visited.

[warm] During those six months, you can drive on your own licence. If you're only staying temporarily, you can apply for up to six more months.

[warm] If you've only just arrived, don't panic. Six months is enough time, as long as you start early. It only gets tight if you leave it until month five.

[serious] But after that, you need a German licence. And this is the one part of this lesson to take really seriously. Driving on the foreign licence after the deadline counts as driving without a licence, [[Fahren ohne Fahrerlaubnis]]. That's a criminal offence, not a parking ticket.

> SCENE (term): The translation.
> Übersetzung | translation, or an International Driving Permit

[curious] And while you drive on it: if your licence isn't in German and doesn't follow an international model, you need an [[Übersetzung]], a translation, or an International Driving Permit to go with it.

## Anlage 11: which tests you need

> SCENE (fact): The list that decides it.
> Anlage 11 FeV | Fahrerlaubnis-Verordnung | Annex 11 of the Driving Licence Regulation: which countries, which classes, which tests.

[curious] So, will you have to take a test? That depends on one list: [[Anlage 11]], Annex 11 of Germany's Driving Licence Regulation. It names countries, and for each one, the licence classes. There are three cases.

> SCENE (list): The three cases.
> Keine Prüfung | listed for class B: tests waived @ first
> Nur Theorie | theory test only @ second
> Beide Prüfungen | not listed: theory and practical @ third

[warm] The first: your country is listed for class B with no tests. Japan, Switzerland and the United Kingdom are examples, and, since August 2026, Ukraine. You apply, and the tests are waived.

[curious] The second: your country is listed as "theory test only". You take the theory test, and the practical test is waived.

[warm] And the third: your country isn't listed. That's true of many countries, so you're in good company. You take both the theory and the practical test. Driving lessons aren't compulsory then, so you don't sit through a beginner's full course. But you do take the test in a driving school car.

[curious] One detail catches people out. Some US states and Canadian provinces are listed individually. So "the United States" isn't the question. Your state is.

> SCENE (fact): The caveat.
> Die Liste ändert sich. | Anlage 11 | Check the current text, and ask your Führerscheinstelle before you book anything.

[serious] And please hear this part: [[die Liste ändert sich]]. The list changes. Before you book anything, check the current text of Annex 11, and ask your local licensing office. What a friend was told three years ago may not be true for you today.

## Applying, and what to bring

> SCENE (term): The office.
> Führerscheinstelle | the licensing office where you live

[warm] You apply at the [[Führerscheinstelle]], the licensing office where you live. And apply as early as you can. Processing often takes weeks, and your six months don't wait for the paperwork.

> SCENE (words): What you usually bring.
> Licence, Translation, Passport or ID, Meldebescheinigung, Photo, Eye test, First-aid course

[curious] What to bring, usually: your original foreign licence. A translation, for example from the ADAC or a sworn translator. Your passport or ID. Your registration certificate, the [[Meldebescheinigung]]. A biometric photo. An eye test. And usually a certificate from a first-aid course.

[warm] That's the usual list, not the final word. Your office tells you exactly what it needs. And when you receive the German licence, you hand in the foreign one.

## If you take the tests

> SCENE (term): The driving school.
> Fahrschule | driving school

[curious] If you need the tests, you sign up with a driving school, a [[Fahrschule]]. The school registers you for the test with the TÜV or DEKRA. And you can sit the theory test in German or in one of twelve other languages, among them English, Turkish, Arabic, Russian and Polish. They're the same thirteen languages as the app. So if your German is still growing, that's no reason to wait.

> SCENE (list): What works differently here.
> Rechts vor links | right before left @ right before left
> Schulterblick | a shoulder check on every turn @ shoulder check
> Tempo 30 | thirty zones @ thirty
> Stoppschild | a full stop, every time @ stop sign

[firm] Now, a word for experienced drivers. Even if lessons aren't compulsory, take a few. The test looks for things that work differently elsewhere. Right before left. A shoulder check on every turn. Thirty zones. And a full stop at every stop sign.

[dry amusement] Twenty years of driving is a real advantage. Twenty years of habits from somewhere else, less so.

[warm] The good news: this series has already covered them. Right of way is lesson one, and the practical test and its manoeuvres are lessons twelve and thirteen.

## What it roughly costs

> SCENE (stat): Rough fees.
> 40 | € office fee, 40–50 @ office
> 25 | € theory test @ theory
> 130 | € practical test @ practical

[curious] And the money. Fees vary by city and by driving school, so treat these as a rough guide. The office fee is around forty to fifty euros. A translation, thirty to sixty. The theory test, about twenty-five. The practical test, about a hundred and thirty.

[warm] On top of that come the driving school's costs: registration, any lessons, and the car on test day. It's worth comparing a few schools.

[curious] And if your case needs no tests at all, most of that list falls away. Then it's mainly the office fee and the translation.

## Your steps, in order

> SCENE (list): The way through, step by step.
> Anmelden | register where you live @ register
> Anlage 11 | check your case @ check
> Antrag | at your office, early @ apply
> Prüfungen | only if your case needs them @ tests
> Umtausch | new licence in, old one out @ hand in

[warm] So here's the whole way through, in order. First, register where you live; that starts your six months. Then check your case in Annex 11. Apply at your licensing office, early, with your documents. Take the tests, if your case needs them. And finally, collect your German licence and hand in the old one.

[curious] Five steps. And if your country is in the first case, step four simply disappears.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The four things from this lesson.
> EU & EWR | stays valid @ EU
> Sechs Monate | then a German licence @ six months
> Anlage 11 | decides the tests @ Annex 11
> Früh beantragen | apply early @ early

[warm] So: from the EU or the EEA, your licence stays valid. From anywhere else, six months from the day you take up residence, then you need a German one. Annex 11 decides which tests you take, and the list changes, so check it and ask your office. And apply early.

> SCENE (outro): The key line, then the app.
> Sechs Monate. | Six months, then a German licence. | Umschreibung

[warm] If you have to take the theory test, you can practise every question in the free app, in English, in German, or in any of the thirteen languages the test is offered in. The link is in the description.
