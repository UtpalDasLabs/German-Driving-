## Cold open

> SCENE (traffic): A three-lane motorway: the blue car on the acceleration lane, a lorry and a car on the main carriageway.
> mw01

[curious] Ask anyone abroad what they know about German roads, and you'll hear the same thing. The Autobahn. No speed limit. Foot down, as fast as the car will go.

> SCENE (stat): The advisory speed.
> 130 | km/h: the Richtgeschwindigkeit

[dry amusement] It's half true. Large stretches really have no general limit. But many have signs, and everywhere else there's a recommended top speed: [[Richtgeschwindigkeit]], 130. Crash well above it, and you can be given part of the blame, even when it wasn't your fault.

[warm] And speed is the least of it. The motorway has its own rules about joining, stopping and queues. Plus one rule that can save a life.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Autobahn

[serious] This lesson covers the motorway, and then the hazards that come for every driver: rain, ice, fog, night, wild animals, and your own tiredness. Twenty-six questions. Let's go.

## On and off

{Qmw01}

> SCENE (question): Question mw01. The blue car on the acceleration lane, signalling left.

[curious] You're joining the motorway. What's correct?

[countdown] Use the acceleration lane to match the motorway speed. And traffic on the motorway has priority. Both are right. The acceleration lane is for reaching their speed and finding a gap. Crawling onto a motorway is dangerous.

[firm] The trap: nobody has to let you in. Plenty of drivers move left if they can. That's courtesy, not a duty.

{Qmw10}

> SCENE (question): Question mw10. The expressway sign: a white car on blue.

[curious] This blue sign with the white car marks an expressway, a [[Kraftfahrstraße]]. What applies here?

[countdown] Only motor vehicles built for over 60 km/h may use it. And at junctions, traffic on the expressway has priority. Think of it as a little motorway: fast vehicles only, no stopping, no turning round. And no pedestrians, on the edge or anywhere else.

{Qmw07}

> SCENE (question): Question mw07. The countdown marker with three stripes.

[curious] Leaving is easier. Before every exit, these markers count you down. How many metres before the exit is the one with three stripes?

[countdown] 300 metres. Then two stripes at 200, one at 100. A hundred metres per stripe. When you see three, you should already be heading for the right-hand lane.

{Qmw08}

> SCENE (question): Question mw08. White marker posts along the roadside.

[curious] And the white posts along the edge, the [[Leitpfosten]]. How far apart do they normally stand?

[countdown] 50 metres. Worth knowing, because they're a free measuring tape. Use them to judge gaps. And keep that number in mind: it comes back when we get to fog.

## What you never do

{Qmw03}

> SCENE (question): Question mw03. A motorway, the hard shoulder empty.

[firm] Now the things that are simply off limits. What is forbidden on the motorway?

[countdown] Turning round and reversing. And stopping, even on the hard shoulder, except in an emergency. Slip roads included. Overtaking on the left, of course, is exactly what that lane is for.

[dry amusement] Missed your exit? Then the next exit is your exit now.

{Qmw09}

> SCENE (question): Question mw09. The hard shoulder beside a queue.

[firm] So who may drive on the hard shoulder?

[countdown] Nobody - unless signs open it to traffic. On some busy stretches, overhead signs do open it at rush hour. Otherwise it isn't a lane. It's for breakdowns and the emergency services. Being stuck in a queue gives you no right to it, however tempting it looks.

## When it all stops

{Qmw05}

> SCENE (question): Question mw05. Brake lights ahead: the tail of a queue.

[curious] You come over a hill, and ahead, everything is standing still. The tail of a queue, a [[Stauende]]. What do you do?

[countdown] Brake in good time and switch on the hazard lights. And leave enough room to the last vehicle. The hazards warn the people behind you. The gap gives you somewhere to go if one of them brakes too late.

> SCENE (term): The rule that clears the way.
> Rettungsgasse | the emergency corridor | StVO § 11

[serious] Now, the most important rule in this lesson. The [[Rettungsgasse]], the emergency corridor. Picture it. Somewhere ahead, a crash. Someone is trapped, and minutes matter. Behind you, an ambulance. And in front of it, three lanes of stopped cars, bumper to bumper.

[warm] There's only one way through: everyone makes room before it arrives.

{Qmw02}

> SCENE (question): Question mw02. A queue on a three-lane motorway; an ambulance comes from behind.

[firm] A queue is forming on the motorway. Where do you form the emergency corridor?

[countdown] Between the far left lane and the lane next to it. The far left lane moves left. Everyone else moves right. However many lanes there are.

[curious] An easy way to remember it: hold up your right hand. Your thumb is the far left lane. Your fingers are all the others. The corridor is the gap between them.

[serious] And do it as soon as traffic slows to walking pace, not when you hear sirens. By then there's no room left to move. The hard shoulder isn't the corridor: there may be a broken-down car on it, and the rescuers need the gap where they expect it.

{Qmw04}

> SCENE (question): Question mw04. A car with hazard lights on the hard shoulder.

[curious] Now it's your car that stops. You break down on the motorway. What do you do?

[countdown] Switch on the hazard lights and, if possible, get onto the hard shoulder. Put on a hi-vis vest and get everyone behind the crash barrier. And set up the warning triangle about 200 m back. All three, in that order: yourselves first, then the triangle. Everyone out on the passenger side.

[warm] The triangle distances: 50 metres in town, 100 on country roads, 200 on the motorway.

{Qmw06}

> SCENE (question): Question mw06. A car coming the wrong way, in the left lane.

[serious] And the warning nobody wants to hear on the radio: a wrong-way driver, a [[Falschfahrer]], on your stretch. What do you do?

[countdown] Keep right and do not overtake. And slow down and increase the gap. The wrong-way driver usually thinks they're in the right-hand lane. Which means they're coming at you in the left one. Stay out of it, and if they get close, hazards on.

## Rain, ice and wind

> SCENE (sign): Three warning signs for the weather and the wild.
> 114 | 117 | 142

[curious] Now, the hazards. These signs warn of three of them: [[Schleudergefahr]], a slippery road. [[Seitenwind]], crosswind. And [[Wildwechsel]], wild animals. But the weather doesn't always come with a sign.

{Qha03}

> SCENE (question): Question ha03. A dry road, the first drops of rain.

[curious] It starts to rain after a long dry spell. What's true?

[countdown] The road can be especially slippery now. Weeks of dust, rubber and oil mix with the first rain into a greasy film. Only longer rain washes it away. So the first shower is the worst one.

{Qha01}

> SCENE (question): Question ha01. Heavy rain; the steering goes light.

[firm] Heavy rain, and the steering suddenly goes light. That's aquaplaning: your tyres are riding on water. What do you do?

[countdown] Declutch or come off the accelerator. And hold the steering straight. Your tyres have no grip, so braking and steering do nothing, until the grip comes back. And then the car goes wherever the wheels are pointing. Foot off, hands still.

{Qha02}

> SCENE (question): Question ha02. A bend in winter; ice on the road.

[curious] Where should you especially expect ice in winter?

[countdown] On bridges. And in forest sections and shaded stretches. A bridge has cold air above and below it, so it freezes first. In the shade, ice stays longer. Even when the road before it was dry.

{Qha14}

> SCENE (question): Question ha14. A snowy road.

[firm] So when do you need winter tyres?

[countdown] In black ice, packed snow, slush, ice or frost. Germany has no fixed dates. The rule follows the weather. In those conditions, you need winter or all-season tyres with the Alpine symbol, a mountain with a snowflake. Plain M+S tyres stopped counting in October 2024.

[warm] The rule of thumb is [[von O bis O]]: from October to Easter, Ostern.

{Qha10}

> SCENE (question): Question ha10. A gust from the side; the crosswind sign.

[curious] On a bridge or at a gap in the trees, a crosswind hits you. What do you do?

[countdown] Slow down and hold the wheel firmly. And expect a gust when passing lorries. Crosswind comes in gusts. Calm, small corrections keep you straight. Jerky steering makes the car weave.

{Qha04}

> SCENE (question): Question ha04. A road in thick fog.

[curious] Fog. When may you switch on the rear fog light?

[countdown] When fog cuts visibility below 50 m. And then the limit is 50 kilometres an hour. Remember the marker posts? Fifty metres apart. If you can't see the next one, you're under fifty. In rain, or just at night, the bright red light only dazzles the drivers behind you.

## At night

{Qha13}

> SCENE (question): Question ha13. A country road at night, main beam on.

[curious] A country road at night, main beam on. When must you dip?

[countdown] When someone comes the other way. And when I am close behind another vehicle. In fact, anywhere you'd dazzle someone, cyclists and pedestrians included. Not just for lorries.

{Qha05}

> SCENE (question): Question ha05. Oncoming headlights at night.

[curious] And when someone dazzles you?

[countdown] Look at the right-hand edge of the road. And slow down, stopping if necessary. After a dazzle, your eyes are nearly blind for a few seconds. And at speed, a few seconds is a long way. Flashing back only means two blind drivers.

{Qha06}

> SCENE (question): Question ha06. A deer in the headlights, on a bend.

[serious] At night, a deer is standing in your headlights. What do you do?

[countdown] Dip the headlights, sound the horn and brake. Main beam freezes wild animals. Dipping and the horn drive them off. And if a collision cannot be avoided: hold the wheel firmly and do not swerve dangerously.

[serious] That second part is hard to accept. But a tree, or an oncoming car, is far more dangerous than the deer. [[Abblenden, hupen, bremsen]]. Don't swerve.

{Qha07}

> SCENE (question): Question ha07. A deer on the road after a collision.

[curious] And if you did hit it?

[countdown] Secure the scene. Then inform the police or the hunting tenant. The police will usually contact the hunter for you. Your insurer will want a certificate of the wildlife accident.

[dry amusement] And leave the animal where it is. Taking it home would be poaching, however good the venison looks.

## Reading the road

{Qha08}

> SCENE (question): Question ha08. A right-hand bend ahead, with its warning sign.

[curious] A bend ahead. When do you brake for it?

[countdown] Before the bend, while still going straight. In the bend, your tyres need all their grip to hold the line. Brake there, and the car can slide straight on. Slow in, steady out.

{Qha16}

> SCENE (question): Question ha16. Following a lorry on a country road.

[curious] You're following a lorry on a country road. Why keep a bigger gap?

[countdown] So I can see past it down the road. And because the lorry driver may not be able to see me. Right behind it, you sit in its mirrors' blind spot. A simple test: if you can't see their mirrors, they can't see you. And of course, lorries do brake. Sometimes suddenly.

{Qha11}

> SCENE (question): Question ha11. Turning left; a motorbike approaching from ahead.

[serious] You want to turn left. A motorbike is coming towards you. What makes this tricky?

[countdown] Its speed is hard to judge. And it is easily overlooked because it is so narrow. A motorbike looks further away and slower than it is. Many crashes happen exactly like this. The trap answer gets it backwards: the motorbike has priority, like all oncoming traffic. When in doubt, wait.

{Qha15}

> SCENE (question): Question ha15. An ice-cream van at the roadside.

[warm] An ice-cream van has stopped at the roadside ahead. What do you expect?

[countdown] Children suddenly running across the road. Wherever something pulls children in, an ice-cream van, a playground, a school, they run without looking. So you slow down and get ready to brake.

{Qha12}

> SCENE (question): Question ha12. A phone ringing in the car.

[firm] Your phone rings while you're driving. What may you do?

[countdown] Talk hands-free without picking the phone up. You may not pick it up or hold it, not even at a red light. Not even when start-stop has cut the engine. Only when the engine is fully off are you not driving.

> SCENE (term): The microsleep.
> Sekundenschlaf | microsleep | 3 s at 100 km/h: over 80 m

[serious] Last, the hazard you bring along yourself. [[Sekundenschlaf]], microsleep. Your eyes close for three seconds. At a hundred kilometres an hour, that's over eighty metres driven blind.

{Qha09}

> SCENE (question): Question ha09. A long, straight road at dusk.

[curious] You're getting tired on a long drive. What really helps?

[countdown] Take a break and, if possible, a short sleep. Only sleep cures tiredness. An open window and loud music buy you a few minutes, at most. And driving faster just gets you to the crash sooner.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The four rules of this lesson.
> Motorway traffic first | merge at their speed @ priority
> Rettungsgasse | far left goes left, the rest go right @ corridor
> Never turn, reverse or stop | the next exit is yours @ next exit
> Slow down before trouble | bends, fog, ice, tiredness @ slow down

[warm] So, that's the motorway and its hazards. Traffic on the motorway has priority. The emergency corridor goes next to the far left lane. You never turn, reverse or stop. Missed it? The next exit is yours. And when trouble is coming, you slow down before it, not in it.
