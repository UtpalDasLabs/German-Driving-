## Cold open

> SCENE (traffic): A junction with a green light for the blue car. In the middle stands a police officer, arms out to the sides, chest towards the blue car.
> sg04

[curious] Picture this. You're coming up to a junction, and your light is green. Lovely. But in the middle of the junction stands a police officer. Arms stretched out. Chest turned towards you.

[warm] The light is saying one thing. The person in uniform seems to be saying another. And you have about two seconds to decide which one you believe.

[firm] In Germany, that's not a matter of opinion. There's a ranking, and it's written into the law. By the end of this video, you'll know it by heart.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> Ampel, Polizei, Blaulicht

[warm] This lesson is about everything that tells you to stop or go: traffic lights, police officers, and the flashing lights on emergency vehicles. Fifteen questions like the ones in the test. Several of them are worth five points, so they're worth getting right.

## Who outranks whom

{Qsg04}

> SCENE (question): Question sg04. The green light, and the officer signalling stop.

[curious] Let's settle the opening straight away. Your light is green. A police officer signals you to stop. What do you do?

[countdown] Stop, the police take precedence. The officer can see things the traffic light can't: an accident round the corner, a convoy, a fire engine on its way. A light runs on a timer. The officer is reacting to what's actually happening.

{Qsg05}

> SCENE (question): Question sg05. Four sources of rules, in four possible orders.

[curious] So what's the full ranking? Which order of precedence is correct?

[countdown] Police, traffic lights, signs, general rules. Each one beats everything below it. The light overrides the priority signs, and the signs override right before left.

> SCENE (list): The order of precedence, top to bottom.
> Polizei | the person @ person
> Ampel | the light @ light
> Schilder | the sign @ sign
> Regeln | the rule @ rule

[warm] The app has a four-word way to remember it: person, light, sign, rule. The higher it stands, the more it knows about this exact moment.

## The officer's arms

> SCENE (term): The officer's rule, as one line.
> Brust oder Rücken: Halt | chest or back to you: stop | StVO § 36

[curious] Officers don't need a hundred hand signals. There are only a few, and the main one goes like this: [[Brust oder Rücken: Halt]]. If you see the officer's chest or back, that's a red light for you. If you see their side, it's green.

{Qsg01}

> SCENE (question): Question sg01. The officer faces the blue car, arms out to the sides.

[curious] Try it. The officer is facing you, with both arms stretched out to the sides. What does that mean for you?

[countdown] Stop before the junction, like a red light. Think of the outstretched arms as a barrier across your path. Traffic in front of and behind the officer stops. Traffic moving along the line of the arms may go.

{Qsg02}

> SCENE (question): Question sg02. The same officer, but the blue car comes from the side.

[curious] Now you come from the side. The arms point along your direction. What does that mean?

[countdown] I may go, like a green light. Nobody needs to wave you through. The arms are pointing the way you're already heading. And if you turn, the normal turning rules still apply.

{Qsg03}

> SCENE (question): Question sg03. An officer with one arm raised straight up.

[firm] One more signal, and this one has two right answers. The officer raises one arm straight up. What does that mean?

[countdown] Anyone before the junction waits for the next signal. And anyone already in the junction clears it. The raised arm is the officer's amber: attention, things are about to change. So nobody new drives in on a raised arm.

## The traffic light

> SCENE (sign): The traffic-lights-ahead warning sign.
> 131

[curious] Now the traffic light, the [[Ampel]]. Everyone thinks they know it. Red, amber, green. But the test likes the moments in between.

{Qsg08}

> SCENE (question): Question sg08. The light shows red and amber together.

[curious] Your light shows red and amber together. What does that mean?

[countdown] Green is coming, I still wait. In Germany, the light goes red, then red and amber, then green. The amber is a "get ready", not a "go". Rolling off now is jumping the light.

{Qsg07}

> SCENE (question): Question sg07. The light turns amber as the blue car approaches.

[curious] You're driving towards a green light, and it changes to amber. What do you do?

[countdown] Stop, if I can still do so safely. Amber means wait before the junction. If you're so close that braking would be dangerous, you carry on.

[dry amusement] What amber never means is "accelerate". It's not a challenge.

{Qsg13}

> SCENE (question): Question sg13. A green light, but cars queue beyond the junction.

[firm] Now a trap. Your light is green, but the traffic is queuing on the far side of the junction. What do you do?

[countdown] Wait before the junction. If you'd have to stop inside it, you mustn't drive in, green or not. Otherwise you're still standing there when the cross traffic gets green, and now nobody can move. In German: [[Kreuzung freihalten]], keep the junction clear.

{Qsg14}

> SCENE (question): Question sg14. Green for both directions. The blue car signals left; an oncoming car and a pedestrian.

[curious] You have green, and you want to turn left. What do you need to watch for?

[countdown] Let oncoming traffic go first. And pedestrians crossing the road I am turning into. A green light doesn't switch off the turning rules from lesson one. Left-turners still wait. Only a green arrow inside the light itself means your turn is protected.

> SCENE (term): The green arrow sign.
> Grünpfeil | the green arrow sign | StVO § 37

[curious] And then there's the little metal sign that confuses everyone: the [[Grünpfeil]]. A green arrow on a black background, fixed next to the red light. It's not part of the light. It doesn't glow.

{Qsg06}

> SCENE (question): Question sg06. A red light with the green arrow sign beside it; the blue car wants to turn right, a pedestrian is crossing.

[curious] Your light is red, with that green arrow sign next to it. You want to turn right. What applies?

[countdown] I must first stop at the stop line. Then I may turn if I do not obstruct or endanger anyone. The app's tip puts it well: the green arrow is a stop sign that lets you turn. Full stop, look, and pedestrians and cyclists with green always go first.

## When the lights go dark

> SCENE (traffic): The junction with its lights switched off, a give-way sign at the blue car's road, a car from the right.
> sg09

[warm] Sometimes the light does nothing at all. A power cut, a fault, or it's switched off at night. That's when you find out whether you read the signs on the way in.

{Qsg09}

> SCENE (question): Question sg09. The lights are dark; a give-way sign stands at the blue car's road.

[curious] The traffic lights are out of order. What applies now?

[countdown] The priority signs at the junction. And if there are no signs, right before left. Go back to the ranking. With the light gone, the next level down takes over. Here, that's the triangle, so you wait.

{Qsg10}

> SCENE (question): Question sg10. A single amber light flashing; a priority-road sign at the blue car's road.

[curious] And a single amber light, flashing on and off at a junction. What does that mean?

[countdown] Caution, the road signs apply. A flashing amber light decides nothing. It's just the light saying: I'm not in charge right now, look at the signs. Here, the yellow diamond gives you priority. You'll often see this late at night.

## Blue lights and amber lights

> SCENE (term): Blue light and siren.
> Blaulicht und Martinshorn | blue light and siren | StVO § 38

[serious] Now the lights on vehicles. [[Blaulicht und Martinshorn]], blue light and siren, together. When you see and hear both, someone's life may depend on how quickly you react.

{Qsg11}

> SCENE (question): Question sg11. An ambulance with blue lights and siren behind the blue car; oncoming traffic.

[serious] An ambulance with blue lights and siren is coming up behind you. What do you do?

[countdown] Make way immediately. Not in a minute, not at the next lay-by. Move over to the side as far as you safely can. And don't simply brake and stop in your lane. That leaves the ambulance stuck behind you.

{Qsg12}

> SCENE (question): Question sg12. A police car at the roadside, blue lights on, no siren.

[curious] Now a police car standing at the roadside. Blue lights on, but no siren. What does that mean?

[countdown] It warns of a hazard, such as an accident. Blue light on its own means take care, not get out of the way. Slow down and pass carefully. Something has happened up ahead.

> SCENE (term): The app's tip, as one line.
> Blau und Horn: Platz machen | blue and siren: make way. Blue alone: take care.

[warm] So, [[Blau und Horn: Platz machen]]. Blue and siren, make way. Blue alone, take care.

{Qsg15}

> SCENE (question): Question sg15. A refuse lorry on the road with an amber flashing light.

[curious] Last one. A refuse lorry stands in the road with an amber flashing light on its roof. What does the light mean?

[countdown] It warns of a hazard. Amber on a vehicle gives it no special rights. Here the danger is the crew, stepping out from behind the lorry with a bin. So pass slowly, with plenty of room.

## What to take with you

> SCENE (list): The four rules of this lesson.
> Polizei, Ampel, Schild, Regel | person, light, sign, rule @ person
> Brust oder Rücken: Halt | side-on means go @ chest
> Gelb heißt warten | amber is not a challenge @ amber
> Blau und Horn: Platz machen | blue alone: take care @ siren

[warm] So, that's lights and signals. Person, light, sign, rule. Chest or back means stop, side-on means go. Amber means wait, if you safely can. And blue light with siren means make way, at once.
